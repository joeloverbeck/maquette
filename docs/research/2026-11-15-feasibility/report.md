# Measured proportions solve half the character

Building this repository is feasible, but the working hypothesis holds only halfway. Statistics from competent drawings can set good defaults and slider ranges for a front-view face and coarse body proportions; they cannot supply the two things the earlier attempts lacked, a head that turns convincingly and a drawing that looks designed. Measured proportions decide which category a character reads as (anime rather than realistic, an age band, a build), while eyes, hair masses, line density and the separately designed three-quarter and side views are authored drawings that studios enforce through one person's judgement, not through ratios. No existing system combines anime style, continuous sliders with selectable part types, measured defaults, clean SVG and headless operation; the nearest, a three-month-old MIT repository built with Claude Code, covers front-view SVG and should be run before any code is written. Automatic measurement works today only for near-frontal faces and upper bodies, with a landmark detector whose accuracy nobody has published, so a few hundred hand-labelled drawings are unavoidable. The architecture most likely to work is a small 3D rig of named landmarks that fixes proportions and placement in every view, 2D shapes authored at three key views, and an explicit table of per-view corrections, because it turns "are the proportions right?" into arithmetic an AI agent can test. AniCraft's importer discards group structure, so the hand-off is one SVG per part with its viewport centred on the joint, plus a sidecar file; publishing aggregate statistics is legally low-risk, while the private copying needed to measure, dataset terms and lookalike parts are the exposures. The deciding unknown is not code but who supplies and approves a few dozen designed shapes, since the developer cannot draw and no automated judge of anime appeal is reliable.

## Proportions buy category legibility; appeal and the turning head are authored

This report synthesises six research notes dated 15 November 2026. Nothing in them was built or run, so every statement about a tool's behaviour is a reading of its documentation or code, and the recommendations are judgements drawn from that evidence.

The hypothesis is right that anime proportions can be measured, and several studies have already done it. The most usable one measured 100 animated film characters, Japanese and American, against a human reference, with every length divided by face height. The Japanese characters' **eye opening is 0.16 of face height against 0.06 for a real face (2.7 times)**, their eye area is 3.4 times larger, their nose is 1.6 times narrower, and their lip-to-chin span is shorter at 0.21 against 0.25 ([Liu et al. 2019](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf)). A study of 173 frontal faces from 90 popular series shows which ratios carry a character's age between 10 and 18: eye ratios shrink and the lower face lengthens ([Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022](https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1)). In Western children's animation, head-to-height and eye-to-head ratios track the intended audience age at r = −0.72 and −0.67 ([Carter et al. 2016](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Designing-Animated-Characters-for-Children-of-Different-Ages-Paper.pdf)). Proportions do reliably set the category a character reads as, and these published numbers are enough to seed first defaults without measuring anything new.

The same sources show where the hypothesis stops. There is no single set of good proportions to recover. Japanese drawing lessons put an adult woman at 5.5 to 6.5 heads tall ([Clip Studio lesson, ときお](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152927)), at 6.5 ([Clip Studio lesson, 哀川空](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151156)) or at about 7 ([Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/1722)), and they place the eye line at half the head ([Miyuli](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/156114)) or below it ([AnimeOutline](https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-face-structure/)). Each author is describing their own style family. The measured spread says the same thing: in the film study the eye opening has a standard deviation of 0.08 on a mean of 0.16, so **the spread inside one style is half the average**. A pooled average would also describe the wrong population. The largest illustration corpus "does focus heavily on female anime characters" ([Gwern](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021)), with 8.66 million posts tagged `1girl` against 2.2 million tagged `1boy` and only 51,595 tagged `mature_female` ([Danbooru tags API](https://danbooru.donmai.us/tags.json?search%5Bname%5D=chibi)). Proportions also barely encode a female character's age: the age study placed male characters within one year 44% of the time and **female characters only 17% of the time**. Defaults fitted on a scraped corpus would put a teenage girl at the centre of every slider.

Nor does closer to the average mean better. Averages of two female lead faces were rated more attractive and more protagonist-like than averages of four or eight ([Ota 2024](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kaogaku/24/1/24_3/_article/-char/en)). Children shown altered characters chose the unaltered design 146 times out of 240 and were biased against the enlarged heads that convention prescribes for young audiences ([Carter et al. 2016](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Designing-Animated-Characters-for-Children-of-Different-Ages-Paper.pdf)).

The gap that matters most for the failed head turns is that anime drawings of one character from different angles do not describe one 3D shape. The standard graphics paper on the problem states that "the 2D appearance intended may not correspond to any real 3D shape" and that rendering one model "cannot account for cartoons that have mutually inconsistent appearances in different views" ([Rivers et al. 2010](https://www-ui.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~takeo/papers/alec_siggraph2010_cartoon.pdf)). Productions that start from 3D spend their effort removing the consistency. Arc System Works hand-edits the normals on faces and keeps separate in-battle and close-up models with different face proportions ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140703095/)), and an indie production's rigger deforms the face mesh for profile shots so the mouth reads as anime ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2022-03-02/indie-animation-staff-reveals-gnarly-cg-techniques-used-to-achieve-anime-look/.183151)). **Proportions measured on front views do not determine the three-quarter view (head turned about 45 degrees) or the side view.** Each view is its own design with deliberate departures from geometry, called cheats in the rest of this report, and no source found quantifies them.

Professional practice confirms that proportions are the smaller part. Studios specify a character with drawings, not numbers: a turnaround, an expression sheet and basic action poses ([Japanese Wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/キャラクターデザイン)). A chief animation director then redraws other animators' work, correcting outlines and eyes first and the nose next, and those corrections "can be taken as the definitive answer" ([Sakuga Blog, Kyoto Animation roundtable](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2020/07/22/commanding-kyoanis-animation-character-designers-chief-supervisors-roundtable/)). The levers designers talk about are line count against visual richness, and the density of detail in the eyes and hair that makes a character memorable ([Real Sound interview](https://realsound.jp/book/2024/02/post-1581975_2.html)). Instructional books map temperament and age to named eye variables: outer-corner tilt, lid droop, iris size, highlight count and lash scheme ([Clip Studio, Mynavi excerpt](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152284)). Colour is a separate standardised track with its own designer ([Sakuga Blog glossary](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/glossary/)). One Kyoto Animation designer keeps eyes simple to "avoid elements in character sheets that invite such errors", a rule that transfers directly: simple parts stay valid across a slider's whole range. Making a woman read as thirty and not sixteen shows the mix, in a bundle the notes assembled from craft lessons because nobody has measured it. It takes a longer lower face and a higher head count, which are proportions, and also a narrower eye with a sharper outer corner, a smaller iris with fewer highlights and more lash drawing ([Clip Studio, Mynavi excerpt](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152284)), with only the nose tip indicated ([Clip Studio lesson](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152504)), which are drawing choices. No study found measures adult women's anime faces; the age study stops at 18.

Taken together the evidence divides the work four ways. This split is the research notes' synthesis, and no study has tested the hypothesis directly by rating corpus-derived characters against designed ones. Measured proportions, stratified by style, age, gender presentation and view, cover reading as anime, the apparent age band, build, and bounds that stop sliders producing broken faces. A second group is not proportion but is still a set of numbers: an eye described by a couple of dozen values, nose and mouth as fractions of the eye-to-chin span, an age slider that moves several values together, and a detail slider for lash strokes, strand lines and highlights. A third group needs a library of designed parts: hair, eye style families, mouth shapes per view, face outlines per view. The fourth needs a person: choosing the style family, curating what it is fitted on, and judging whether the whole face works. The statistics literature fits this picture. In caricatures, 32 principal components recover 99% of the variation in 63 facial landmarks, so landmark geometry is low-dimensional; but exaggeration depends on the direction of a face's offset from the mean, not its size ([CariGANs](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.00222)), and a basis built on realistic faces does "not work well for extrapolation" into stylised ones ([3D-CariGAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06841)). The workable form is a discrete style centre with a small linear model and data-bounded ranges around it, not one global mean.

## One young repository covers front-view SVG; measured defaults and multiple views remain unbuilt

Consumer anime avatar makers are not parametric. Picrew composes characters from artist-uploaded layered images ([Picrew support](https://support.picrew.me/en/?p=1642)), and the other 2D makers surveyed follow the same swap-and-nudge model, so none has a notion of jaw shape or eye-line height. The one mainstream tool with real anime proportion sliders, VRoid Studio, is 3D and GUI-only, and its guidelines forbid creating "an application that can generate or output 3D models, avatars, or items" built from its meshes and textures without a separate licence from pixiv ([VRoid Studio Guidelines](https://vroid.com/en/studio/guidelines)). Open SVG avatar generators solved headless, deterministic, JSON-in and SVG-out operation long ago, but for small Western cartoon busts picked by seed, with no bodies and no continuous proportions: DiceBear has 10,090 stars and 61 styles ([DiceBear](https://github.com/dicebear/dicebear); [style licences](https://www.dicebear.com/licenses/)), and faces.js has been active since 2012 under Apache-2.0 ([faces.js](https://github.com/zengm-games/facesjs)).

Anime-specific generators drawn in code barely exist. GitHub keyword searches on 15 November 2026 for "parametric anime face", "anime svg parametric", "anime avatar generator svg" and similar phrases returned zero repositories ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=parametric+anime+face&type=repositories)). The one real match is `rhedak/anime-character-creator`: MIT, TypeScript, created on 5 August 2026, 5 stars. It describes itself as "Anime-style characters, drawn entirely in code", with every shape "placed against a small skeleton of proportion anchors". It has a dependency-free headless CLI and about a thousand tests that fail on any byte change, and its README says it "was built with AI coding agents ... mostly Claude Code" ([repository](https://github.com/rhedak/anime-character-creator)). Its API exposes **22 mostly continuous face fields and about ten body fields**. Its own documentation states the limits: "there is no second eye shape to choose", defaults are tuned to the project's own reference art, 19 of its 21 presets are a 2.4-head "tall chibi", and its adult body was "traced from reference images" ([API reference](https://github.com/rhedak/anime-character-creator/blob/main/docs/api.md); [README](https://github.com/rhedak/anime-character-creator/blob/main/README.md)). No view-angle parameter appears in its API reference, so it appears to be front-only. That reading comes from its documentation; the research did not run it.

Two other projects come close on a different axis. Mannequin Character Generator is a paid, closed-source desktop tool (USD 29.99, early access) that exports anime character portraits as SVG and as Inochi2D puppets; its pages mention no CLI or scripting and do not say whether shapes move on continuous sliders ([itch.io](https://ar14.itch.io/mannequin)). Hamr is a 12-star, six-month-old "headless-first, agent-orchestrated" parametric anime generator, but it builds 3D avatars through Blender, not vectors, and its capabilities are known only from its README ([Hamr](https://github.com/hrabanazviking/Hamr)). The matrix below is the research notes' reading of these sources.

| System | Anime | Continuous sliders | Selectable part types | Measured defaults | SVG out | Headless | Multiple views |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rhedak/anime-character-creator | yes | yes (22 face, about 10 body) | hair and outfit only | no | yes | yes | appears front-only |
| Mannequin | yes | not confirmed | yes (templates) | no | yes | no | poses; angles not confirmed |
| Hamr | yes | yes | presets | no | no (3D) | yes | yes (3D) |
| VRoid Studio | yes | yes | yes | no | no | no | yes (3D) |
| faces.js | no | not confirmed | yes | no | yes | yes | no |
| FaceGen | no | yes | no | **yes** | no | SDK | yes (3D) |

The Skyrim and Oblivion comparison in the brief covers two different designs, and the difference matters. Oblivion's heads used FaceGen data files, per modding documentation ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/oblivion/mods/20166?tab=docs)). FaceGen is the direct precedent for statistically grounded sliders: a face is a mean plus principal-component modes, "currently 50" symmetric and 30 asymmetric, each scaled to one standard deviation; named sliders are directions in that space; random faces are draws from a normal distribution; and a "caricature" control is distance from the mean ([FaceGen SDK manual](https://facegen.com/dl/sdk/doc/manual/indepth.html)). The manual concedes that it models gender as one hump where the real distribution has two, the same flaw a single anime average would have. Skyrim's sliders are artist-made morph files, per a modding forum ([Nexus forum](https://forums.nexusmods.com/topic/13507926-resource-for-creating-vanilla-facegen-sliders-for-custom-races)), and anime-styled creators took that authored route with large preset libraries: Code Vein was counted at nearly 500 eyebrow options, 58 hairstyles and 66 eye highlights ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/code-veins-character-creation-has-all-the-options-1838495058)). The cheapest workable hybrid is Nintendo's Mii format, in which each feature is a part index plus a few transform values such as scale, rotation, spacing and height ([3dbrew](https://3dbrew.org/wiki/Mii)). The practical form for 2D anime is the mixture the brief already describes: authored, nameable parameters whose defaults and ranges are fitted to measurement.

The routes that skip a parametric model do not deliver what this project is for. Tracing a generated image yields dense colour regions with no part identity: the leading SVG model's character data, partly made by generating images and tracing them, averages 28,000 tokens per character with groups and transforms stripped out ([OmniSVG](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06263v3)). StarVector states that it "will not work for natural images or illustrations" ([StarVector](https://github.com/joanrod/star-vector)). A benchmark of 22 language models writing SVG directly found that "all models exhibit systematic performance degradation with increasing complexity" ([SVGenius](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03139)), which matches the earlier experience of an agent placing Bézier points by hand. The real machine-learning competitor for animatable 2D anime characters is raster layer decomposition toward Live2D, such as See-through, which splits one illustration into up to 23 inpainted layers ([See-through](https://github.com/shitagaki-lab/see-through)). It handles any art style but gives one view, no sliders, raster output and no rigging yet. A parametric system's niche is what those routes lack: the same part structure in every character and view, exact editability, small files and deterministic output.

The first thing to study is therefore `rhedak/anime-character-creator`: run it, read its architecture notes, and decide whether to fork it, contribute to it, or treat it as a detailed comparison. It is the strongest reason not to start from zero. It does not settle the matter, because it is young, single-author and house-styled, with no measured layer, one eye construction and no evident second view. Beyond it, the FaceGen manual is the specification for the statistical layer, faces.js is the model for a JSON character file with a CLI and partial-specification completion, the Mii format is the minimum parameter scheme, and the 2.5D paper discussed below is the model for views. Picrew, CHARAT and VRoid assets cannot serve as a base.

## Frontal faces measure automatically; skull tops, profiles and legs need hand labels

Supply of drawings is not the constraint. Danbooru carries 1.33 million posts tagged `full_body`, 1.37 million tagged `standing`, 65,432 tagged `straight-on` and 187,596 tagged `profile` ([Danbooru tags API](https://danbooru.donmai.us/tags.json?search%5Bname%5D=full_body)). Its human tags are precise but incomplete: "almost all tags on images are correct, but the absence of a tag is often wrong" ([Gwern](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021)). That is the right failure mode for selecting by framing and view, and the wrong one for age, where the tags are sparse enough that filtering on them returns the drawings in which the trait was striking enough to tag.

The instruments are the constraint. Detecting anime faces, heads and bodies is a solved commodity under an MIT licence ([deepghs/imgutils](https://github.com/deepghs/imgutils)). Locating landmarks (named points such as the chin or an eye corner) is not. The only maintained model, `hysts/anime-face-detector`, "detects near-frontal anime faces and predicts 28 landmark points" and publishes neither an accuracy figure nor a description of its training data ([repository](https://github.com/hysts/anime-face-detector)). Its points cover the jaw outline (5), the brows, the eyes (6 each), one nose point and the mouth (4), with **no point for the top of the head, the hairline, the ears or the pupils** ([demo notebook](https://github.com/hysts/anime-face-detector/blob/main/demo.ipynb)). The only anime or manga landmark model with published error, a 60-point manga model from 2018, removed profile faces from its data and still failed on 19.31% of an easy subset: frontal faces of at least 80 pixels with both eyes visible ([Stricker et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.03214)). For bodies, the anime-specific pose estimator places noses, eyes and ears within half a head length 98 to 99% of the time and shoulders 90%, but elbows 73% and **hips, knees, wrists and ankles only 63 to 67%**, on single full-body characters and with no awareness of perspective ([Chen and Zwicker](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.01819)). General pose models do worse on drawings unless retrained on art: cartoon pose accuracy rose from 37.6 to 64.7 AP with art-domain training ([Human-Art](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.02760)).

Multimodal language models are no substitute for a detector. On the main pointing benchmark, where a hit only requires landing inside the target's mask, GPT-4o scored 29.5% and Gemini 2.5 Flash 46.8% ([PointArena](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09990v2)), and the best specialised pointing model of 2026 reached 70.7% ([MolmoPoint](https://allenai.org/blog/molmopoint)). Measuring proportions needs error of a few percent of face size, far tighter than that test, and no evaluation of any such model on anime landmarks was found. Their plausible role is triage: confirming that a drawing is a single, standing, front-facing, uncropped figure.

The table gives the notes' verdict per measurement. The verdicts are estimates reasoned from each instrument's published specification; none is a measured result for this pipeline.

| Measurement | Automatic route today | Verdict | Hand work needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eye spacing, eye width | 28-point landmarks on near-frontal faces | Obtainable | Validation set only |
| Eye height and roundness | Same | Obtainable, noisier (short distance; lashes and half-closed eyes bias it) | Validation; filter out expressions |
| Mouth-to-chin and lower-face length | Same | Obtainable, noisy; the nose point is the least trustworthy | Validation |
| Eye line on the head; head height | Landmarks plus a head box or hair mask | Biased: it measures to the top of the hair, not the skull | Mark the cranium top on a calibration subset, or define the parameter against hair top |
| Jaw and chin shape | Five jaw points are too sparse; a skin mask from a part parser is unvalidated | Not reliable | Hand-drawn contours on a few hundred faces |
| Heads tall | Head box plus character mask on solo, standing, full-body, frontal figures | Aggregate only, biased low by hair | Check a few hundred; treat chibi separately |
| Shoulder width | Shoulder keypoints | Obtainable for front-facing torsos | Validation |
| Leg length ratios | Hip, knee and ankle keypoints | Marginal, aggregate only | Hand labels recommended |
| Arms, hands, feet, neck, waist | None reliable | Poor | Manual |
| Any three-quarter or side proportion | No landmark model supports it | Not automatic | Manual, or 3D avatars with their own bias |

Two consequences follow. With 5,000 to 50,000 drawings per stratum, random error averages out. What does not average out is systematic offset (hair inflating the head, residual head turn, foreshortening, clothing) and selection toward the drawings detectors handle: frontal, standing, simply dressed, modern. Slider ranges are harder than means because they come from the tails of the distribution, where detectors fail most, so robust quantiles such as the 5th to 95th percentile are safer than extremes. More images fix neither problem. The fix is a hand-labelled reference set of **roughly 300 to 500 drawings** marked with exactly the points the creator's parameters need, including cranium top, hairline, jaw contour and heel. It quantifies each automatic measurement's bias and directly supplies the parameters that have no automatic route. The scale is realistic: the published anime age study was done by hand on 173 images ([Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022](https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1)).

Three-quarter and side views are the hard case for data as well as for drawing. No curated public dataset of studio model sheets was found. The practical raw material is Danbooru's 5,060 `turnaround` and 23,432 `reference_sheet` posts ([Danbooru tags API](https://danbooru.donmai.us/tags.json?search%5Bname%5D=turnaround)), which mix fan and official sheets, hold several views per image and carry no view labels. Measuring 3D anime avatars removes detector noise but samples avatar-tool templates, not hand-drawn practice.

A permissively licensed pipeline exists for the frontal work: Danbooru metadata for filtering, the deepghs detectors, the hysts landmarker, a character mask ([SkyTNT](https://github.com/SkyTNT/anime-segmentation)) and an art-trained pose model, with See-through's 19-part body parser as an unvalidated option for hair, face and neck boundaries. Two components need care. The anime pose estimator's code is AGPL-3.0 ([bizarre-pose-estimator](https://github.com/ShuhongChen/bizarre-pose-estimator)), which is fine for private measurement and not for linking into a published tool, and the Human-Art data is restricted to non-commercial use ([Human-Art](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/HumanArt)). No published study has extracted proportion statistics from a large anime corpus with these tools, so the pipeline's feasibility is assembled from component benchmarks and is untested as a whole.

## A 3D landmark rig with authored key views makes consistency testable

Four architectures were weighed. Pure 2D templates per view are the easiest to code, and nothing in them keeps the views in agreement: every slider's effect must be authored once per view, and no test can say whether "eye line 10% higher" means the same thing from the side. Deriving lines from a 3D mesh keeps views consistent but produces the wrong drawing. Reliable contour extraction from meshes was still being solved in a 2023 research paper ([ConTesse](https://dgp.toronto.edu/~hertzman/contesse/)), Blender's SVG exporter documents that its layer ordering "is by no means perfect" ([Blender manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.1/addons/render/render_freestyle_svg.html)), and one practitioner sums up anime outlines in Blender as a choice between "lack of control", "lack of performance" and "pixelization" ([Blender Artists](https://blenderartists.org/t/learn-how-to-use-blender-freestyle-for-anime-outlines-master-class/1328573)). That route also moves the hard part to sculpting a parametric anime head mesh, which an agent places no more reliably than Bézier points. Warping a single front drawing, as Live2D does, reaches only about 30 degrees either side ([Live2D](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-editor-manual/standard-parameter-list/)), so it cannot produce a side view.

The architecture the evidence supports sits between these. In the 2.5D cartoon model of Rivers, Igarashi and Durand, "a stroke's shape is determined by 2D interpolation across its key views, while its position and Z-ordering are determined by its associated 3D anchor position", and "just three or four key views" per stroke gave models that rotate to any angle ([Rivers et al. 2010](https://www-ui.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~takeo/papers/alec_siggraph2010_cartoon.pdf)). The recommended hybrid extends that idea in three layers. First, a small 3D rig of named landmarks (chin, jaw corners, eye centres, brows, nose tip, mouth, ears, cranium) is the single source of truth. Sliders move landmarks, and every view is a rotation and flat projection of the same points, so **proportions and placement agree across views by construction**. Lines fixed on the head, such as brows and the hairline, can be stored as 3D Bézier curves, because a flat projection of a cubic Bézier is exactly the cubic Bézier of its projected control points; one published representation uses this split of fixed 3D curves plus view-dependent contours ([3Doodle](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.03690v1)). Second, shapes that are silhouettes or stylised symbols (the head outline with its cheek and jaw, the eyes, the hair) are 2D shapes authored at front, three-quarter and side and blended between those views. Third, an explicit table of per-view corrections holds the cheats.

Cheats have had an accepted encoding since 1999: store view-specific corrections keyed on camera direction and blend them ([Rademacher 1999](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall00/cs597b/papers/rademacher99.pdf); [Fukusato and Maejima 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15472)). Blender artists sell an add-on that drives shape keys from camera angle to fix anime profiles that do not match their fronts ([CamKeys](https://blendermarket.com/products/camkeys)). Three mechanisms cover every case in the notes. A continuous offset fades in with the angle, for the far cheek's bulge and for a far eye that narrows less than geometry says. A discrete swap over a range of angles handles the profile mouth and nose, which Rivers et al. treat as "complementary groups of strokes". A draw-order override handles hair locks that pass in front of or behind the face. Corrections should be stored relative to the landmark frame so that they ride along when a jaw slider moves.

The 2.5D paper's own stated limits mark where the difficulty will be: interior lines that should merge into the silhouette, sharp or concave shapes, and "Hair is a difficult case for our system". MIT also lists a related US patent, 8,791,942 ([MIT Technology Licensing Office](https://tlo.mit.edu/industry-entrepreneurs/available-technologies/25d-cartoon-models)), and its scope against a design with per-part 3D anchors and interpolated key-view shapes should be checked before publishing. No open-source implementation of the 2.5D model or of this hybrid was found, so there is no reference to measure effort against. The notes' rough estimate is four to eight thousand lines of core logic, excluding authored shapes and tests.

### Eyes and hair are designed parts with sliders, not generated shapes

No published parametric anime-eye generator for vector output was found, so the eye module has to be designed for this project. Standard landmark schemes under-describe anime eyes: one cartoon landmark detector documents that six points per eye force "very round cartoon eyes [into] an almond shape" ([MDFLD](https://github.com/zenonLEE/MDFLD)), and the closest prior project has a single eye construction. The notes propose 20 to 30 numbers per eye (its box, tilt, an upper lash line with a thickness profile, the extent of the lower lid, iris proportions, pupil, and the count and placement of highlights) plus a small set of discrete style families. The far eye in three-quarter view is an authored correction, and the side-view eye is a different shape swapped in. Because outlines and eyes are what professionals correct first, they deserve the tightest constraints and the most review time. A handful of well-designed eye presets will do more than wide random ranges.

Hair is the hardest part to make look designed. Every source that handles anime hair well builds it from separate strands along guide curves: VRoid Studio's hair editor does ([VRoid help](https://vroid.pixiv.help/hc/en-us/articles/360012339194-Hair-Editor)), and a 2025 generative model trained on 37,000 anime hairstyles represents each hair card as a sequence of control points ([CHARM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21114)). The fitting 2D form is a hairstyle as a list of clumps, each a 3D guide curve rooted on the scalp plus a width profile and a layer (behind the head, on it, or in front of the face), projected per view. That keeps views consistent and avoids the concave shell that defeats interpolation. It does not create design. The realistic plan is a small library of hand-designed styles with a few sliders each (length, volume, fringe, parting), not a hairstyle generator.

### Bodies are cut-out rigs and lines are filled outlines

For flat vector output, a skeleton with slider-driven bone lengths and a width profile per limb segment is simpler than adapting a 3D body model. Cut-out animation practice separates the joint hierarchy from the draw order ([Godot](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/3.0/tutorials/animation/cutout_animation.html)). Limbs must be split at the joints so the two halves can sit on opposite sides of the torso, and garments become shapes bound to the same skeleton and split into front and back pieces. Hands, feet and loose clothing need their own templates and are the largest content cost after hair.

SVG has no variable-width stroke, so tapered anime lines must be emitted as filled outlines built from a centreline and a width profile. The Rust library kurbo provides stroke expansion, curve offsetting and cubic fitting under Apache-2.0 or MIT, but neither variable-width stroking nor Boolean operations ([kurbo](https://docs.rs/kurbo/latest/kurbo/)); perfect-freehand builds pressure-style outlines under MIT ([perfect-freehand](https://github.com/steveruizok/perfect-freehand)). Because the model emits Bézier curves directly, no tracing step is involved, which is its main quality advantage over any raster route.

### The agent verifies with arithmetic and a person approves a few frozen references

The generator knows its own landmarks, so the reliable checks need no detector. An agent can assert target ratios such as eye line over head height, eye width over face width and heads tall. It can assert left-right symmetry of the front view; that each landmark in every view equals the projection of its 3D point plus the declared correction; that every slider moves landmarks monotonically and within bounds; that outlines do not cross themselves; that the iris stays inside the eye; and that every control point moves continuously as the head turns, except at declared swaps. A second tier compares renders with human-approved reference images, and the closest prior project already works this way with its byte-exact tests.

Perceptual tools are weakest exactly where the project is hardest. The anime landmark detector is near-frontal only. The cartoon detector reports that "profile views and covered eyes are still hard" ([MDFLD](https://github.com/zenonLEE/MDFLD)). Anime aesthetic scorers publish no validation against human ratings ([aesthetic-shadow-v2](https://huggingface.co/shadowlilac/aesthetic-shadow-v2)), and a 2026 study found that language-model judges of SVG "respond unevenly across error types and SVG styles" ([SVG-Score](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03806)). These tools are useful as smoke tests for gross failures such as an eye outside the head, and none should gate a design. Human approval therefore remains necessary at a few points: the base head in each key view, each correction table, and each eye and hair preset. The design goal is to make those approvals few and to freeze each one as a reference that tests protect.

## AniCraft flattens groups, so structure travels as part files plus a sidecar

These findings come from reading AniCraft's source and documentation in a local checkout (version 0.3.0, commit d439e3e, at `~/src/anicraft`); the links below point at the same files in its upstream repository. Nothing was imported or run. AniCraft's SVG importer keeps leaf geometry, each leaf's `id` as the element name, fills, strokes, gradients, transforms, and clip paths as mask layers. It does not keep structure. Group elements are traversed and not preserved, `<use>` references are expanded into unlinked copies, anything with `display:none` is discarded, and `<style>` sheets are not read ([SVG importer source](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/crates/svg/src/lib.rs)). A whole SVG imported to the stage lands on one layer. Imported to the Library it becomes one graphic symbol named after the file, and **the symbol's registration point is the centre of the SVG's viewport** ([import command source](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/crates/engine/src/cmds_file.rs)). A single well-grouped character file therefore arrives as a flat pile of named shapes with no pivots.

The hand-off that fits is the one Flash animators use. The generator should emit one SVG per animatable part, with the file name as the part name and the `viewBox` centred on the part's joint (the shoulder for an upper arm, the base of the neck for a head), because that is the one mechanism found that carries a pivot through the import. Every leaf path needs a unique `id` of at most 128 characters with the hierarchy encoded in the name. Styling must be inline, with no filters, patterns, embedded images or gradient strokes. Alternates must never be hidden, since hidden mouth shapes and blink states are dropped; each goes in its own file. Clips belong inside the part that needs them, such as an iris clipped by the eye white. Tapered lines can travel as filled outlines, or as uniform strokes whose width profile a script applies afterwards, since AniCraft has native variable-width strokes that its importer cannot read from SVG ([ADR 0018](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/docs/adr/0018-variable-width-strokes.md)).

Alongside the parts, the generator should write a sidecar JSON holding the skeleton, each part's file, parent bone, pivot, draw order per view and set of states, plus named expressions and suggested spring values for hair chains, which AniCraft's bones support for secondary motion ([bones guide](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/docs/guide/masks-guides-camera-bones.md)). AniCraft is command-driven from a CLI and an MCP server ([headless guide](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/docs/guide/working-headless.md)), and its `bone.add` command accepts joint coordinates ([command reference](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/docs/guide/reference/commands.md)). The generator can therefore also emit a command script that imports the parts, creates layers, places instances, fills a mouth symbol's frames and adds bones, so the animator opens a ready rig. AniCraft's built-in templates show the structure it expects: a turnaround of five views (front, three-quarter, side, three-quarter back, back) and a mouth chart of ten labelled shapes ([templates source](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/crates/doc/src/templates.rs)). Its README still reads "Early alpha: not yet for production animation work" ([README](https://github.com/storytold/anicraft/blob/d439e3e10e38eda630aa413871b76f721a2a0719/README.md)), which argues for keeping the sidecar tool-neutral and the script thin.

The art itself has to be built for motion, and here a parametric generator starts ahead, because it already knows what automatic rigging research works to recover from flat images: separate layers, hidden regions, draw order and joints. Live2D's guidance, the most explicit for anime faces, asks for hair split into front, sides and back; eyes split into lashes, eyeball and white; the mouth split into upper lip, lower lip and interior; and hidden areas painted in, such as the face under the hair and a neck long enough to reach the mouth ([Live2D tutorial](https://docs.live2d.com/en/cubism-editor-tutorials/psd/)). For a generator that means every fill continues under whatever covers it, and every limb segment ends in a round cap centred on its joint. By the notes' arithmetic, a bust that can blink, talk and emote needs about 30 named parts and a full body about 45, with three to ten mouth shapes, four eye states (open, half, closed, smiling) and three to five brow shapes per view. Head turns should be emitted as discrete key views that share part names and a registration point at the base of the neck, which is how Flash animators build heads with several angles ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-540/does-anyone-know-how-to-create-a-rig-with-multiple-head-angles-107406)). Lip-sync timing is available downstream from Rhubarb Lip Sync under MIT, whose shape letters map onto frames of a mouth symbol ([Rhubarb](https://github.com/DanielSWolf/rhubarb-lip-sync)).

RPG use is two products with different proportions. Map sprites sit on small fixed grids. RPG Maker MZ characters are "normally 48x48 pixels" in four directions of three patterns each, with faces of 144 by 144 pixels ([RPG Maker MZ help](https://rpgmakerofficial.com/product/MZ_help-en/01_11_01.html)), and the Liberated Pixel Cup layout uses frames of 64 by 64 pixels with an eight-frame walk ([LPC generator constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LiberatedPixelCup/Universal-LPC-Spritesheet-Character-Generator/master/sources/state/constants.ts)). A figure eight heads tall in a 64-pixel frame has a head about 8 pixels high, too small to carry anime eyes. The generator therefore needs **a separate chibi preset** of two to three heads with thicker limbs and lines and larger eyes, defined as a full parameter set and not as a scaled-down model. Guilty Gear's developers did the equivalent, building in-battle models with different face proportions from the close-up models so characters stay readable when small ([4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140703095/)). Equipment should follow the LPC composition model, in which each wearable is a part with a numeric z-position and a list of the animations it supports ([LPC body definition](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/LiberatedPixelCup/Universal-LPC-Spritesheet-Character-Generator/master/sheet_definitions/body/body.json)); in a parametric generator the art per body type becomes a function of the body's parameters. Portraits are the animation bust again: aligned layers named by group and attribute serve Ren'Py's layered images ([Ren'Py](https://www.renpy.org/doc/html/layeredimage.html)) and crop to RPG Maker faces. True pixel-art output needs grid-aligned geometry and a fixed palette that a general SVG rasteriser does not give, so smooth-scaled sprites and portraits are the realistic first targets. No rig file format needs to be written now: AniCraft already exports Lottie, sprite sheets and video, and the raster runtimes surveyed would all consume per-part images plus the same sidecar.

## Aggregate statistics are free to publish; copies, dataset terms and lookalike parts are not

This section is background research from public sources, not legal advice, and no court in the United States, the European Union or Japan has ruled on measuring artworks to derive design statistics. The published output is on settled ground in all three. US copyright does not extend to "any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery" ([17 U.S.C. §102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)), and "the raw facts may be copied at will" ([Feist v. Rural](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340)). The EU's directive on text and data mining notes that mining "mere facts or data that are not protected by copyright" needs no authorisation ([Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)). Japan's Copyright Office states that protection applies to creative expression "and not to the 'idea' itself" ([Agency for Cultural Affairs](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)). A mean eye height across thousands of drawings by many artists is a fact about a population. The closest US authority treated a doll's exaggerated features as an unprotectable idea, in Mattel v. MGA as read through a secondary source ([University of Michigan Library](https://guides.lib.umich.edu/substantial-similarity/mattel)). Style and genre conventions are likewise unprotected as a general rule, while a specific character that is "especially distinctive" with "unique elements of expression" is protected ([DC Comics v. Towle](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2015/09/23/13-55484.pdf)).

The act of measuring is where the three systems differ, because it requires copies. Japan is clearest. Article 30-4 permits use for data analysis, defined to include "statistical analysis of the constituent language, sounds, images, or other elemental data from a large number of works", where the purpose is not to enjoy the works' expression, and subject to a proviso against unreasonably prejudicing the copyright owner. The research notes read this as covering commercial analysis. The official guidance warns against targeting a small group of one creator's works, circumventing access controls, and knowingly collecting from sites that distribute infringing content ([Agency for Cultural Affairs](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)). The EU permits mining by anyone under Article 4 where access is lawful and the rightholder has not reserved the use by machine-readable means, with copies kept only "for as long as is necessary" ([Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)). The US has no statutory exception and relies on fair use. Non-generative analysis resembles the book scanning held fair in Authors Guild v. Google ([US Copyright Office summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/authorsguild-google-2dcir2015.pdf)). Against that, on 29 September 2026 the Third Circuit affirmed a ruling against a non-generative legal search tool, reasoning that "ease is not a justification for copying"; the opinion is sealed and was read only through law-firm alerts ([BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/what-thomson-reuters-v-ross-does-and-doesnt-say-now-about-fair-use-and-generative-ai/)). The source of copies matters as well: a library built from pirated books was held not to be fair use, in a case that ended in a USD 1.5 billion settlement ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)). The notes rate private downloading for measurement as low risk in Japan, low to medium in the EU, and medium and uncertain in the US. Which law governs depends largely on where the developer does the downloading, a question the research did not settle.

Dataset terms bind independently of copyright. Full Manga109 "can be used solely for academic purposes" with redistribution forbidden ([Manga109 project](https://manga109.github.io/manga109-project-website/en/)), which rules it out for a solo developer. Manga109-s, 87 of the 109 volumes, expressly permits commercial use of results from image-processing experiments, on application, with acknowledgement and no redistribution ([Manga109-s](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hal-utokyo/Manga109-s)). **It is the cleanest large input**, though it is monochrome manga and not colour illustration or anime. Danbooru-derived corpora are the weakest. The site's terms place responsibility for content on the uploader ([Danbooru terms](https://danbooru.donmai.us/terms_of_service)), a CC0 or MIT label added by a dataset packager cannot relicense the artists' images, and the maintainer of the Danbooru2021 dataset took it offline citing "the changed legal climate" and noting that "it is impossible to assure that every single image is legal to possess" ([Gwern](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021)). pixiv's guidelines prohibit "Use of crawlers or other programs to aggregate works" ([pixiv terms](https://policies.pixiv.net/en.html)). MakeHuman's assets are CC0 and safe to measure or incorporate ([MakeHuman licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/master/LICENSE.md)), but they are realistic humans.

The largest practical exposure is the part library and its presets, the only place the project authors specific expression. A stock feature drawn afresh is safe. A part that reproduces the particular drawn form of one character's signature element, or a set of parts that reassembles one character, is not, and character names add trademark and unfair-competition exposure on top. A further point matters for a workflow driven by an agent. Under the US Copyright Office's 2025 report, "Copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material, or material where there is insufficient human control over the expressive elements", and "prompts do not alone provide sufficient control" ([US Copyright Office, Part 2](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). A character whose parts and slider values an AI agent chose from a short instruction gives its owner a weak claim to that selection. A person setting the values in a deterministic tool is on the assistive side of that line, though protection for a bare selection is likely thin.

The safe-practice rules follow from these positions. Compute every published statistic over many works by many artists and eras, never over one artist, series or character. Keep no landmark sets, parameter vectors or presets tied to an identifiable character or drawing, and discard measurements per image once they are aggregated; the same discipline applies to fitting view corrections against turnaround sheets, where only a table averaged over many sheets should survive. Prefer clean inputs: commissioned drawings with a written licence covering analysis and commercial use of results, CC0 material, Manga109-s, and the conventions (not the diagrams) in drawing manuals, with scraped boards last. Honour robots.txt, rate limits, logins and opt-outs. Measure in memory where practical, keep the corpus private, and never publish images, crops or traced outlines. Draw parts from the aggregate statistics and the designer's own ideas, keep dated authorship records, and name nothing after existing characters, series, studios or artists. Screen each commercial character against well-known ones before release. License the part artwork as CC0 or with an explicit output exception, as MakeHuman does, so composed characters belong to their maker. Keep parameter files and edit history as evidence of human choices, record data provenance in a short statement, protect names by trademark, and take advice specific to the developer's jurisdiction before a commercial launch.

## Prove a turning head on published numbers before measuring a corpus

The recommended system has five layers. A character is a small file of part choices and slider values, separate from any geometry, as in FaceGen. A 3D landmark rig and a skeleton turn those values into positions in any view. Each feature is a selectable part type with a handful of continuous parameters, the Mii scheme extended with true curve morphing for the jaw and eyes, with shapes authored at front, three-quarter and side and a correction table for the cheats. A stroke layer turns centrelines and width profiles into filled outlines. An emitter writes an illustration SVG, part SVGs, a sidecar and an optional AniCraft script. The statistical layer sits on top as data, not code: for each style family, a centre and ranges expressed in standard deviations, bounded by the observed spread of a stratified sample, seeded from published numbers and set by hand where data is thin.

The order of work should follow risk, and the highest risks are not the ones a measuring plan addresses. The developer's conclusion that nothing should proceed until the base proportions are right is sound, but base proportions are the cheapest item to get right. Once they are named ratios with tests, the published values are enough to start: a face divided 0.29, 0.37, 0.12 and 0.21 from hairline to chin, an eye opening of 0.16 and a nose width of 0.12 of face height ([Liu et al. 2019](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf)), and adult head counts from the tutorial presets. The earlier failure at the turning head was a view-design problem that no corpus statistic would have fixed. The first stages therefore test the head in three views on existing numbers, the first shippable output is card art, and large-scale measurement comes afterwards as a refinement whose main products are ranges and style-family centres. The staging below is this report's recommendation, built on the notes' risk ranking.

| Stage | Build | What it settles | Exit test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Run the rhedak repository; import one hand-made part SVG into AniCraft; hand-label 100 drawings and compare with the landmark detector | Fork or build; whether the pivot survives import; how accurate the instrument is | Decisions written down |
| 1 | Head landmark rig, flat projection and ratio tests, drawn as dots and guide lines in front, three-quarter and side | Base proportions as arithmetic | All ratio and projection tests pass |
| 2 | Head outline with jaw and cheek at three key views, neck, jaw sliders, first correction table | The turning head, where earlier attempts failed | A person approves three views and a slider sweep; frozen as references |
| 3 | Eye module with two or three style families and per-view forms; brows, nose, mouth and ears with swaps | Whether the face reads as appealing anime | A person approves each preset |
| 4 | Stroke emitter, part SVGs, sidecar, one hairstyle, a bust | First usable card art and the AniCraft hand-off | The bust assembles in AniCraft from the script |
| 5 | Measurement pilot: a stratified frontal corpus plus 300 to 500 hand labels; defaults and ranges per style family | The hypothesis, at the scale it can be tested | Bias quantified per measurement; ranges set from robust quantiles |
| 6 | Hair library as clumps on 3D guides | The hardest aesthetic problem | A person approves each style in three views |
| 7 | Skeleton, limb profiles, hands and feet, clothing, chibi preset, sprite sheets | RPG use | Sheets render on the RPG Maker and LPC layouts |
| 8 | Optional: corrections fitted to many turnaround sheets; a principal-component layer for random plausible characters | Refinement | None required |

Stage 2 is the decision point. If a three-quarter head cannot be brought to an approved state there, more data will not rescue it, and the shapes have to come from a person who can draw.

Several things should be decided or checked before starting. The first is fork or build, after running the rhedak repository; the implementation language follows from that choice, since that project is TypeScript and kurbo and AniCraft are Rust. The second is a single style family and era to target, because every statistic and every part belongs to one. The third is who approves the look and where the key-view shapes come from. The developer noticed the earlier failures unaided, which is the judgement the approval role needs; what is missing is a source for corrected shapes. The options are an agent drafting against references with the developer approving, a small commission for a head in three views, a few eye styles and a few hairstyles under a written licence, or fitting to many turnaround sheets later. The notes single out view corrections as the piece an agent working without references is least likely to get right. A 3D anime model rendered offline can also initialise landmark positions per view, with the caveat that it encodes one tool's template. The fourth is the AniCraft smoke test, since the pivot-by-viewport mechanism is an untested reading of code. The fifth is calibrating the landmark detector against hand labels before trusting any number from it. The sixth is the corpus and the jurisdiction the downloading happens in, including a Manga109-s application if that corpus is wanted. The seventh is the patent check and the licence for part artwork.

The main risks are these. No study has tested the hypothesis, no reference implementation of the hybrid exists, and none of the candidate tools was run. The three hardest problems in the notes' ranking are aesthetic ones with weak automated verification: hair that looks designed, three-quarter corrections that look right, and eyes that stay appealing across a slider's range. The number of key views an anime head needs is unknown, because the figure of three or four comes from simpler Western cartoon characters, and the join where the jaw line runs into the cheek silhouette is a known weak spot. The pivotal measuring instrument has no published accuracy, the bias from hair covering the skull has never been quantified, and no tool was tested on chibi figures. The corpus skews young, female and recent, so the strata for mature adults will be thin and need hand-set defaults. AniCraft is early alpha and its import behaviour could change. The rhedak project could add views or eye types within months, and Mannequin's real capabilities are unverified. US fair use for the copying step is unsettled, with several cases pending. Finally, a parametric system trades stylistic range for consistency: it will produce one house style well, not the variety of an image generator.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the order the hypothesis implied. The plan was to measure first and draw from the measurements; the evidence says to make the drawing testable first and use measurement to tune it. Proportions bound the design without supplying it: they keep sliders inside what readers accept as anime, and their real statistical value lies in ranges and style-family centres, which no existing anime tool has. The scarce resource is a few dozen approved shapes, the head outline in three views, a handful of eye styles and hairstyles, and each one, once approved and frozen, is protected by tests from then on. That is a far smaller art requirement than drawing characters, and it is the one place where spending money on an artist, or time on careful approval, buys the most.

The same structure that makes views consistent also makes the output animatable. Named parts, joints, hidden regions and alternates are what automatic rigging research spends large models recovering from flat images, and a parametric generator has them for free; that structural knowledge, more than any single picture it draws, is what distinguishes it from image generation and what AniCraft and game engines can consume. Several agent-built repositories that draw anime characters in code appeared during 2026, so a front-view generator is no longer new. What remains unbuilt is narrower and harder: a head that turns, a library of part types, and defaults that can be traced to evidence.
