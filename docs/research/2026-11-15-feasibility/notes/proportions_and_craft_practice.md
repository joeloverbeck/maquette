# Anime/manga character proportions and professional craft practice: what is measured, what is lore, and what proportions cannot fix

Research date: 2026-11-15. Labels used on every finding:

- **[P]** = I read the primary page/PDF text in this session. **[S]** = taken from a search-result summary or a third-party summary; the primary page was not read (treat as a lead, not as verified).
- **[measured]** = quantitative study. **[lore]** = instructional craft rule (no measurement behind it). **[practice]** = practitioner testimony. **[method]** = technical/graphics paper.

## 1. Published construction conventions with numbers (head counts, face placement, body landmarks)

### Takeaway
Instructional sources agree on the *structure* of the rules (head count rises with age; eyes one eye-width apart; nose and mouth subdivide the lower face) but disagree on the *numbers* by about half a head to a full head for adults and by a factor of two on shoulder width, and almost none of them give a numeric eye-line position or eye size for anime faces. The only numeric face ratios for anime that I could verify come from a measurement study (section 2), not from how-to-draw material.

### Cited Findings

**Head count (頭身, total height in head lengths)**
- [lore][P] Real-human growth canon as stated on Japanese Wikipedia: 1 yr = 4 heads, 4 yr = 5, 8 yr = 6.5, 12 yr = 7, 16 yr = 7.5, adult = 8, elderly = 7. Deformed styles: 2頭身 (head : rest about 1:1), 3頭身 (1:2), 4頭身 (1:3), all called スーパーデフォルメ / ちびキャラ — [ja.wikipedia 頭身](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/頭身)
- [lore][P] "女性は6.5頭身", "男性は7頭身", "子どもは4から5頭身", model-like slim figure "8頭身" — [哀川空, Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151156)
- [lore][P] Women "5.5～6.5頭身前後", attractive men "6.5～7.5頭身" — [ときお, Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152927)
- [lore][P] "A well-balanced human body is about 7-8 heads tall"; worked examples: adult man 7.5, adult woman about 7, senior man about 6, baby about 3 — [Clip Studio Tips, MANABI JOURNEY #3](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/1722)
- [lore][P] A long-running anime blog states that "manga and anime much prefer a 6.5-head-tall character as its baseline", against a Western figure-drawing standard of 7–7.5 and heroic 9; its sources are an Anime Expo 2012 workshop and Wikipedia, and its own three hand measurements (about 6.5 each) were disputed by a commenter who got about 8, 7+ and about 6 for the same characters — [Ogiue Maniax](https://ogiuemaniax.com/2012/07/10/anime-and-idealized-figures/)
- [lore][S, low-reliability blog] Genre chart: shōnen about 6.5–7, shōjo 7–8, seinen 7.5–8.5 heads — [otakukart](https://otakukart.com/4-basic-proportion-rules-can-teach-beginners-how-to-draw-manga/)

**Body landmarks**
- [lore][P] Crotch at half of total height; knee halfway between crotch and feet; wrist at crotch level; elbow at the navel (male) or waist (female); female waist higher than male; "Think of the width of the shoulder as about 1.5 heads width", with a currently fashionable narrow style of about 1 head — [Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/1722)
- [lore][P] Eight-circle construction: neck, shoulders, armpits and chest apex all inside circle 2; waist at elbow height between circles 3 and 4; crotch at the 4/5 boundary (body midpoint); knee at mid-leg so upper and lower leg are equal — [哀川空](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151156)
- [lore][P] Real upper : lower leg is "ほぼ１：１" but manga lengthens the lower leg for a slimmer look — [ときお](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152927)
- [lore][P] Torso length to the leg root = 3 x (top of head to base of neck); male shoulders "頭の幅よりずっと広め", female "頭の幅よりちょっと広め"; male waist low, female waist high; female hips wider than chest; male silhouette rectangular, female flaring downward — [平井太朗, Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152121)
- [lore][S, low-reliability] Shoulder width in head *widths*: male 2–2.5, female 1.5–2; hips male about 1.5, female 1.8–2; waist male 1.2, female 1 — [otakukart](https://otakukart.com/4-basic-proportion-rules-can-teach-beginners-how-to-draw-manga/). Another site gives male 2.0–2.3, female 1.5–1.8, hands about 0.75 head units — [skyryedesign](https://skyryedesign.com/?p=12322)
- [lore][S] General figure-drawing rule (not anime-specific): hand (wrist to fingertip) is about face length (chin to hairline); foot is about one head length; upper arm and forearm about 1.5 head lengths each — [fiveable drawing foundations](https://fiveable.me/drawing-foundations/unit-10/proportions-human-body/study-guide/bVYcw96jRRpCUZJk)

**Face placement**
- [lore][P] Realistic adult baseline: "目・瞳は顔の半分の高さ"; one eye-width between the eyes and one eye-width from each eye to the face outline; deformed faces "can sometimes" reuse the same ratios — [Miyuli, Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/156114)
- [lore][P] "The gap between the eyes is one eye long. The nose sits halfway between the eyes and the chin. The bottom of the lips sit halfway between the nose and the chin." — [Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/1722)
- [lore][P] Anime-specific: "Place the eyes below the horizontal halfway point of the head"; one eye apart; nose halfway between top of eyes and chin; mouth slightly above halfway between nose and chin; ear height can equal the distance from top of eyes to nose; irises "often vertically stretched" — [AnimeOutline](https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-face-structure/)
- [lore][P] Anime head: eyes on the head's middle line "or below it, depending on style"; "A classic proportion tells us to leave a one-eye-wide space between the eyes, but this rule is often broken for a stylized effect"; nose halfway down the lower half; lower half in thirds with the mouth usually on the first line; ear between middle line and nose line; hairline at half of the upper face. Male differences are qualitative only: lower/thicker brows, narrower eyes, minimal lashes, small circular pupil, more pronounced jaw and nose, thicker neck — [M. Zagrobelna, Envato Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-anime-heads-and-faces--cms-31884)
- [lore][S] One tutorial raises the eye line above halfway for male characters — [lunarmimi](https://lunarmimi.net/?p=22170)
- [lore][P] Eye spacing "間に目がひとつ入るくらい"; about 2/3 of the eyeball is hidden by lids; women's brow-to-eye distance is usually longer than men's; the same article states explicitly that an anatomically correct drawing is not necessarily an attractive manga drawing — [いしだわかこ, Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151502)
- [lore][P] Handsome-male face: outer eye corner aligns roughly with the top of the ear; male eyes are a "horizontal rectangle"; to make a man look younger move eyes and nose slightly down and leave the mouth in place — [ときお](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152927)

**Where sources disagree**
- Adult woman: 5.5–6.5 ([ときお](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152927)) vs 6.5 ([哀川空](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151156)) vs about 7 ([Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/1722)) vs 7–8 for shōjo ([otakukart](https://otakukart.com/4-basic-proportion-rules-can-teach-beginners-how-to-draw-manga/)). Adult man: 6.5–7.5 vs 7 vs 7.5.
- Eye line: at half ([Miyuli](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/156114)), "on or below" half ([Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-anime-heads-and-faces--cms-31884)), below half ([AnimeOutline](https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-face-structure/)), above half for men ([lunarmimi](https://lunarmimi.net/?p=22170)).
- Shoulders: 1–1.5 head widths ([Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/1722)) vs 2–2.5 for men ([otakukart](https://otakukart.com/4-basic-proportion-rules-can-teach-beginners-how-to-draw-manga/)).

### Inferences
- The disagreement is itself the finding: there is no single canon. Each author is describing their own style family. A program should treat these as *named presets with ranges* (for example "adult woman: 5.5–7.5 heads, default 6.5") rather than as one truth to be recovered by measurement.
- Part of the shoulder disagreement is probably a unit problem (head *width* vs head *length*, and whether the deltoids are included). Any numeric rule imported from tutorials needs its unit pinned before use.
- The eye-line rule is confounded by what "the head" means in a drawing with large hair volume: half of the *skull* is not half of the *silhouette*. Tutorials rarely say which.
- Rules for nose and mouth are expressed as recursive subdivisions of the eye-to-chin span (1/2, then 1/2 or 1/3). That is directly parameterisable as two fractions.

### Gaps
- I could not extract numbers from the books the brief named (Hikaru Hayashi's "How to Draw Manga" volumes, Christopher Hart, Mark Crilley), nor from Pixiv Sensei, Toshi, or studio training material (アニメーターの基礎, Toei / Kyoto Animation school material). Searches returned only SlideShare scans and blogs; nothing citable was found.
- No source gave neck width, chin length, or eye height as a numeric fraction for anime faces. A search snippet mentioned a male ribcage of 1.5 heads tall by 1.2 wide on a clipstudio.net page, but I could not locate the page, so it is not reported as a finding.
- The Clip Studio / いちあっぷ article on age versus head count (archives/151869) could not be extracted (the fetch tool declined to summarise it), so its numbers are missing.

## 2. Quantitative and statistical studies of anime faces and bodies

### Takeaway
A small number of real measurement studies exist, and they already supply usable numbers: anime film characters have an eye opening about 2.7 times and an eye area about 3.4 times the human reference, a nose about 1.6 times narrower, and a shorter lip-to-chin span, with very large within-style variance. I found no published time series of eye size by decade, no dataset of head-to-body ratios across series, and no published PCA of anime facial landmarks, so those would be new measurements.

### Cited Findings

**Liu, Chen and Chang (2019), "A Study of Facial Features of American and Japanese Cartoon Characters", Symmetry 11(5):664** [measured][P, full PDF text read] — [MDPI PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf), [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.3390/SYM11050664)
- Sample: 100 characters, five faces per film; Japanese films are the top-10 box-office films 2005–2017, American films are Academy-nominated / box-office top-10 / Rotten Tomatoes above 7, same years. 26 location points per face; 13 angles, 16 lengths, 6 areas. Human reference is a symmetric composite of averaged Japanese and American male and female faces. All lengths are divided by face length (hairline to chin).
- Table 3 (fractions of face length; human / Japanese mean (SD) / American mean (SD)):
  - Hairline to brow centre: 0.28 / 0.29 (0.08) / 0.20 (0.09)
  - Brow centre to nose base ("nose length"): 0.35 / 0.37 (0.068) / 0.41 (0.09)
  - Nose base to lip line: 0.10 / 0.12 (0.04) / 0.12 (0.07)
  - Lip line to chin: 0.25 / 0.21 (0.055) / 0.25 (0.11)
  - Eye opening height (upper to lower lid): 0.06 / 0.16 (0.08) / 0.13 (0.07)
  - Skull width: 0.77 / 0.84 (0.17) / 0.68 (0.21); face width: 0.73 / 0.80 (0.16) / 0.78 (0.20)
  - Nose width: 0.19 / 0.12 (0.06) / 0.19 (0.06); ear width: 0.07 / 0.13 (0.12) / 0.10 (0.04)
- Table 4 (areas, same normalisation): eye area 0.005 / 0.017 (0.014) / 0.01 (0.014); nose area 0.03 / 0.02 (0.01) / 0.04 (0.01).
- Authors' summary multipliers for Japanese characters vs human: eye opening 2.7x, eye area 3.4x, nose width 1.6x smaller, nose area 1.5x smaller, ear width 1.9x, face and skull width 1.1x. American characters: eye opening 2.2x, eye area 2x, nose area 1.3x larger.
- The best single discriminator between Japanese and American characters is the nose, not the eye: AUC 0.82 for nose width, 0.78 for nose area. By the tables' own p-values, eye opening (p = 0.12) and eye area (p = 0.30) did not differ significantly between the two.
- Internal inconsistencies I noticed (treat the tables as more reliable than the prose): the text says lip-to-chin is "1.6 times lower" but its own table gives 0.25 vs 0.21 (about 1.2x); Table 1 labels the eye measure "upper and lower eyebrows" while the results text says eyelids; the prose lists eye area among the significant Japan-vs-America differences although Table 4 gives p = 0.2994 for it.

**Bisiecka and Borysławski (2022), Anthropological Review 85(2):123–134** [measured][P] — [journal PDF](https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1)
- 173 frontal images (86 male, 87 female) of characters with canonical ages 10–18 from 90 series chosen by MyAnimeList popularity; landmarks after Martin and Saller (pupil, inner and outer eye corner, nose tip, mouth midline, chin); all measures divided by interpupillary distance; measured in ImageJ.
- With age, eye-related ratios (eye width, eye height) decrease and lower-face ratios (pupil to nose, pupil to mouth, pupil to chin) increase. All measures correlate with age in males; four of six in females.
- Age prediction from these ratios: within one year for 44% of males but 17% of females; off by more than two years for 33% of males and 65% of females. Best single predictor: pupil-to-mouth distance.
- Chin shape (males): 7 of the 9 square chins were in the 16–18 group and none in the 10–12 group (chi-square 10.47, p = 0.03). The authors attribute the poor female result to female characters retaining paedomorphic features.
- Individual r values and regression equations are only in figures and images; no mean ratios are tabulated.

**Carter, Mahler, Landlord, McIntosh and Hodgins (Disney Research, IDC 2016), "Designing Animated Characters for Children of Different Ages"** [measured][P, PDF text read; Western children's animation, not anime] — [paper PDF](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Designing-Animated-Characters-for-Children-of-Different-Ages-Paper.pdf)
- Top three humanoid characters from each of 19 shows/films; correlations with the recommended viewing age: head height / total height r = −0.724 (p = 0.0005); eye height / head height r = −0.672 (p = 0.0016); interpupillary distance / face width r = −0.31 (not significant); eye roundness r = 0.859 (p < 0.00001).
- In the preference experiment children picked the unaltered character 146 of 240 times, were biased *against* enlarged heads (15 of 60), and showed no significant preference for larger, closer-set or rounder eyes; no pattern correlated with the child's age. The authors conclude that artistic convention does not reflect children's actual preferences.

**Liu, Pei and Zhu (2026), "From Pixels to Personas: Tracking the Evolution of Anime Characters", ICWSM 2026** [measured][P, arXiv HTML] — [arXiv 2604.05507](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05507)
- MyAnimeList scrape: 27,783 anime, 138,499 characters, 126,347 avatar images, release years 1917–2025. Visual features are 768-dimensional DINOv2 embeddings reduced by PCA; no landmark or proportion measurements.
- A classifier predicts which of 10 periods an avatar comes from with 39.3% accuracy (majority baseline F1 0.03), so era is partly readable from the face image.
- PC1 is described as moving "from exaggerated masculinity toward softer, more approachable female traits"; PC1, PC3, PC5 and PC7 rise over time, "reflecting greater use of moe elements"; PC3 trends toward slenderness. No effect sizes or eye-size numbers are reported.
- Popularity model: softer, rounder, more feminine faces (PC1) and slender bodies / oval faces (PC3) are among the visual components with the largest contribution, but metadata dominates.

**Other measured or semi-measured work**
- [measured][P, abstract page] Ota (2024), Journal of Japanese Academy of Facial Studies 24(1): average faces built from 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 anime lead characters. For female characters the 2-face average was rated more attractive and more "main-role" than the 4- or 8-face averages, with no monotonic trend; the 32-face female average tended to beat individual faces; no differences for males. Conclusion: main versus supporting role "is judged by some cues in the character's facial features" — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kaogaku/24/1/24_3/_article/-char/en)
- [measured, explicitly "cursory"][P, PDF text read] Rozema, Zakaria and Ní Dhubhghaill (2022), Journal of Optometry: 100 randomly selected animated characters (44 male, 56 female); nearly all have heads larger than normal proportion; pupil size and lid opening are "2–3 times larger than normal reference values", and female characters' pupils are about twice the male ones; Disney princesses' eyes are 1.5–2.5x human — [paper PDF](https://rcastoragev2.blob.core.windows.net/d40209d87c87c0b85b2bba520afb76ca/main.PMC9068580.pdf)
- [measured][P for the three measures and citation] Gould's "A Biological Homage to Mickey Mouse" (in The Panda's Thumb, 1980) measured eye size, head length and cranial vault across Mickey's history — [Genosko, Invisible Culture](https://www.rochester.edu/in_visible_culture/Issue_9/genosko.html). [P for the sentence, attribution uncertain] A 2008 post ("Cartoon Faces", O. Morin) carries the sentence "eye size from 27 to 42 percent of head length; head length from 42.7 to 48.1 percent of body length; and nose to front ear from 71.7 to a whopping 95.6 percent of nose to rear ear", measured with dial calipers on three stages of Mickey's design. The fetch tool read this as the post author's own measurement; from background knowledge I believe it is a quotation of Gould's essay. Not confirmed against the essay itself — [Cognition and Culture](https://cognitionandculture.net/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/621/index.html)
- [measured][S] Chen, Russell, Nakayama and Livingstone (2010): after viewing large-eyed anime faces, observers prefer larger eyes on human faces (adaptation), as summarised in a Japanese thesis abstract — [Hosei repository](https://hosei.ecats-library.jp/da/repository/00030503/23_summary_ota.pdf)
- [measured][S] A 2016 Manga Studies article traces the enlargement of eyes in one shōjo artist's work (五十嵐かおる) — [CiNii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1520290883381577088?lang=en)

**Datasets and tooling that make new measurement feasible**
- [method][S] Manga landmark dataset: 1,446 images with a 60-point scheme adapted from the 68-point human scheme, with extra eye-contour points — [Stricker et al., arXiv 1811.03214](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03214)
- [method][S] 17,920 anime faces with landmarks (Getchu source) — [Landmark Assisted CycleGAN, arXiv 1907.01424](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.01424)
- [method][P, README] An open anime face detector predicts 28 landmarks on near-frontal faces and its README shows a k-means clustering of detected landmark sets — [hysts/anime-face-detector](https://github.com/hysts/anime-face-detector)
- [measured][P] Danbooru2021: 4.9M images, 162M tag instances, 498k tags, 2005–2021 — [gwern.net](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021)
- [S] Japanese vision papers note that anime facial parts differ so much from real faces that standard detectors fail — [University of Tokyo thesis](https://repository.dl.itc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/record/5723/files/K-03735-a.pdf)

### Inferences
- Numbers that need not be re-measured as first-order defaults: vertical face partition for anime film characters (0.29 / 0.37 / 0.12 / 0.21 of hairline-to-chin), eye opening 0.16 of face length, nose width 0.12, skull width 0.84. These are film characters (Ghibli-era box office), so they are a *conservative* anime style; TV moe styles will sit further out.
- The standard deviations are the more important result. Eye opening is 0.16 with SD 0.08 (coefficient of variation about 50%); eye area SD is almost as large as its mean. A single mean is a poor summary of a population this dispersed, and the dispersion is plausibly multi-modal (style families) rather than Gaussian.
- The age study shows which *directions* matter for age (eyes shrink, lower face lengthens, chin squares for males) and that the same cues barely move for female characters. That is direct evidence that an age slider fitted on pooled data will under-age-differentiate women.
- The Disney result is a caution against equating "what artists conventionally do" with "what reads best": convention was measurable, but viewers did not prefer it.

### Gaps
- No study measuring eye size or face shape by decade (1980s to 2020s) with landmarks was found; the only temporal evidence is embedding-based (Liu et al. 2026) or a single-artist study. A CivArchive "average face per year 2008–2025" set exists but its author states it is subjective, not computed.
- No dataset or paper measuring head-to-body ratio across series was found; only anecdotal hand measurements.
- No blog or notebook doing PCA of anime landmark coordinates was found. Averaged-face blog projects were not located by search.
- The Kogakuin (IPSJ 2023) avatar-deformation paper could not be fetched (HTTP 503); only its premise (anime face = large eyes, small nose, small mouth) is known from a search snippet.

## 3. How proportions vary systematically, and whether one "anime average" is a sensible target

### Takeaway
Proportions vary along at least six documented axes (character age, gender presentation, temperament, target audience/genre, era, and even usage context within one production), and the available corpora skew female, young and moe. A pooled average is therefore a blend of incompatible styles weighted toward a teenage girl; an adult woman of about thirty is drawn by moving several cues together, most of which are not pure proportions.

### Cited Findings
- [measured][P] Age: eye ratios fall and lower-face lengths rise with age; female characters change much less than male ones across 10–18 — [Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022](https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1)
- [measured][P] Target-audience age: larger heads, taller eyes and rounder eyes for younger audiences (r = −0.72, −0.67, 0.86) — [Carter et al. 2016](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Designing-Animated-Characters-for-Children-of-Different-Ages-Paper.pdf)
- [lore][P] Eye size encodes age: "目は大きければ大きいほど年齢が若く・幼く"; smaller eyes read as mature. Temperament: drooping outer corner (たれ目) = gentle, raised outer corner (つり目) = strong-willed; drooping lid = sleepy/mysterious; "大人の目" for adult women is long and sharp with many lashes (a made-up look); narrow 切れ長 eyes for stern middle-aged men. Lashes: none = innocent/child, upper only = cute, upper and lower = strongly girlish. Female irises large with more highlights; male irises smaller with restrained highlights, because too much light "looks cute rather than mature" — [Mynavi book excerpt on Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152284)
- [lore][P] Adult woman's face is slimmer than a child's but keeps rounded skull and cheeks; nose relief is gentle and from the front only the tip is indicated; adult man is angular with a clear nose bridge sometimes joined to the brow — [Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152504)
- [lore][S] In childhood the region above the eyes is proportionally larger because the cranium grows before the jaw, and lowering the eyes reads as childlike; by the thirties there is less fullness around eyes and cheeks, and nasolabial lines age a face abruptly if overused — 平井太朗 series, Clip Studio 描き方ナビ: [child face lesson](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151909), [older face lesson](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151933) (search snippets only; which lesson carries which statement was not confirmed)
- [practice][P] Genre and audience change the design brief: for a pre-school audience the designer enlarged the eyes "so they are easy to see", slimmed the legs and made the style more symbolic (記号的); for an idol franchise he kept the established series look and was asked to preserve "キャラを印象付ける目と髪の情報量" — [斎藤敦史 interview, Real Sound](https://realsound.jp/book/2024/02/post-1581975_2.html)
- [measured][P] Era: softer, more feminine and moe-coded faces rise over time; era is predictable from an avatar at 39.3% over ten classes — [Liu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05507)
- [measured][P] National style: Japanese versus American characters differ most in the nose (smaller and narrower in Japan), forehead height and skull width, not in eye size — [Liu et al. 2019](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf)
- [practice][P] Usage context inside one production: in Guilty Gear Xrd the in-battle models differ from the close-up models; "バトル中のキャラクターは顔面が小さく描かれます", and one character's battle model has enlarged eyes so her personality stays readable at small size — [4Gamer, 西川善司](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140703095/)
- [measured][P] Corpus skew: the Danbooru community "does focus heavily on female anime characters"; the top tags are 1girl, solo, long_hair, and so on — [gwern.net](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021)
- [lore][S] In moe styles male characters are drawn with smaller eyes than female ones — [MyAnimeList feature](https://myanimelist.net/featured/1503)
- [measured][P] Averaging is not monotonically good for stylised faces: 2-face averages of female anime leads beat 4- and 8-face averages on attractiveness and "main-role" fit — [Ota 2024](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kaogaku/24/1/24_3/_article/-char/en)
- [lore][P] Head count rises with maturity: women 5.5–6.5 or 6.5, model-like 8 — [ときお](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152927), [哀川空](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151156)

### Inferences
- What designers change to read as a woman of about thirty rather than a teenager, assembled from the findings above (each element is sourced; the bundle is my synthesis): smaller and horizontally longer eye with a sharper outer corner; smaller iris relative to the opening and fewer highlights; more lash and lid drawing (reads as makeup); longer lower face (pupil-to-mouth and pupil-to-chin) and a slimmer cheek contour; eye line not lowered; slightly more nose indication; higher head count (toward 7–8) with a longer lower leg; restrained cheek fullness. Only about half of these are proportions; the rest are drawing choices (lash weight, highlight count, nose mark).
- The measured weakness of age cues in female characters (17% within one year) and the corpus skew toward young female characters together imply that defaults fitted on a scraped corpus will produce a teenage-looking woman at the centre of every slider. Statistics must be stratified by apparent age, gender presentation and style family before they are used as defaults, and the "mature woman" stratum will be thin.
- Because temperament is carried by a few named eye variables (outer-corner tilt, lid droop, iris size, lash scheme), archetype presets are a natural layer on top of continuous sliders.

### Gaps
- No measured comparison of shōjo / shōnen / seinen / josei proportions was found; the genre head-count chart is from a low-reliability blog.
- No measured per-studio or per-designer comparison was found.
- No source measured adult-woman anime faces specifically (the age study stops at 18), so the "thirty-year-old" bundle rests on craft lore plus extrapolation of the 10–18 trend.

## 4. Statistical shape models applied to stylised faces

### Takeaway
For stylised faces, low-dimensional linear models do capture landmark geometry well (32 principal components recover 99% of the variance of 63 two-dimensional landmarks in caricatures), and linear PCA shape spaces are used for cartoon and anime bodies. The documented failures are elsewhere: models built on realistic faces do not extrapolate to stylised ones, PCA blurs texture and line detail, and a linear landmark space says nothing about how the features are drawn.

### Cited Findings
- [method][P, ar5iv full text] CariGANs (Cao, Liao, Yuan, 2018): 63 manually labelled landmarks per face; "We take the top 32 principal components to recover 99.03% of total variants"; data are 10,000 photos and 8,451 hand-drawn caricatures. PCA coefficients are used instead of raw coordinates because raw landmark output produced foldovers and "the face structure is hardly preserved". Exaggeration is a scalar: l_x + α(l_y − l_x) with α in [0, 2]. The characteristic loss preserves the *direction* of a face's offset from the mean face; simply multiplying the offset by 2 or 3 gave unsatisfactory results — [ar5iv 1811.00222](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.00222)
- [method][S] 3D-CariGAN: PCA on 5,343 caricature meshes; 200 components give an explained-variance sum of 0.9997; a PCA basis built from normal faces supports interpolation but does "not work well for extrapolation" into caricature shape space — [arXiv 2003.06841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06841)
- [method][P, abstract] Cai et al. (2020) build a caricature parametric model "on vertex based deformation space" (a nonlinear model, per the title) and regress it from a single caricature image — [arXiv 2004.09190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09190)
- [method][S] 3DCaricShop uses a standard PCA 3DMM as its baseline, combined with implicit reconstruction — [arXiv 2103.08204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08204)
- [method][S] RaBit (CVPR 2023): 1,500 artist-made biped cartoon characters; a SMPL-like linear blend-shape model with PCA for shape, but "directly adopting PCA for texture modeling fails to reconstruct details and falls into blurry results", so texture uses a StyleGAN generator — [CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Luo_RaBit_Parametric_Modeling_of_3D_Biped_Cartoon_Characters_With_a_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- [method][P of a third-party paper summary, not the paper] "Anime-Ready" (ICLR 2026) defines Anime-SMPL, fitted on a private set of 20,000 anime characters, keeping the top 98 principal components; the shape vector covers anime body types and facial proportions; variance explained is not reported — [papernotes summary](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/human_understanding/anime-ready_controllable_3d_anime_character_generation_with_body-aligned_compone/)
- [method][S] FaceGen (realistic faces for games) describes a face with 130 numbers: 50 symmetric shape modes, 30 asymmetric shape modes, 50 colour modes, stored in decreasing order of magnitude — [FaceGen SDK manual](https://facegen.com/dl/sdk/doc/manual/overview.html)
- [measured][P] On anime avatars, PCA of learned image embeddings yields interpretable leading components (facial softness, slenderness / oval face), and the top 50 components were used for prediction — [Liu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05507)
- [method][S] SOAP (2025) registers stylised (cartoon, anime) avatars to a parametric human head model by differentiable deformation rather than building a stylised basis — [arXiv 2505.05022](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05022v2)
- [method][P, README] k-means clustering of detected 28-point anime landmark sets, with the mean image of each cluster, is shown as a demonstration (number of clusters and method details are not given) — [hysts/anime-face-detector](https://github.com/hysts/anime-face-detector)

### Inferences
- A landmark-level linear model of the anime face in a fixed view is very likely adequate with a few tens of components; 20–35 is the range supported by CariGANs (32 of 126 coordinates for 99%). For a slider UI the practical number of *independent, nameable* axes will be smaller, and PCA axes will not align with the semantic axes users want (age, tilt, roundness), so a rotated or hand-defined basis is preferable to raw components.
- The CariGANs "direction not amplitude" result and the 3D-CariGAN extrapolation failure both say the same thing: stylised shape space is not a scaled copy of the realistic one, and sampling far from the mean of a linear model produces invalid faces. Ranges must be bounded by the data per style family, not extrapolated.
- The large SDs in section 2 and the documented style axes in section 3 suggest (but do not prove; nobody has published a cluster analysis with validity statistics) that the population is a mixture. That argues for a discrete style or archetype choice first, then a small linear model around that centre.
- Every model above describes *where* features are. None describes line economy, lash or highlight design, or hair masses; the cartoon work that tried PCA on appearance reports blur. This is the technical counterpart of the craft finding in section 5.

### Gaps
- I found no published 2D statistical shape model (ASM/AAM or landmark PCA) fitted specifically to anime or manga faces, and no dedicated anime head 3DMM with a published basis; the closest is the private Anime-SMPL, known to me only through a secondary summary.
- I did not find component counts or variance curves for any anime-specific model.
- Latent-space approaches (StyleGAN-type anime face generators and direction-finding in their latent space) were not researched to a citable standard in this pass.

## 5. Professional practice in Japan: settei, staying on model, and what is standardised beyond proportions

### Takeaway
Professionals do not standardise a character with numbers. They standardise it with drawings (multi-angle sheets, expression sheets, basic action sheets, colour model sheets) and enforce it by having a chief animation director redraw other people's work, starting with outlines and eyes. The design levers they talk about are line count, the information density of eyes and hair, simplicity that prevents errors, and what the character would or would not do, not ratios.

### Cited Findings
- [practice][P] Anime design must be simple enough to hand-animate, and because production is a many-person division of labour, "三面図・表情集・基本動作集などの詳細なデザインが求められる"; "設定画は以降のプロダクション工程における基礎資料となる"; there is normally one character designer per work, "総作画監督を兼任する場合が多い" — [ja.wikipedia キャラクターデザイン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/キャラクターデザイン)
- [practice][P] Character designers either "have the task of simplifying manga/illustration designs so that they are suitable for animation" or design originals from the director's description; animation directors "check all the key frames being created for an episode and make corrections where necessary"; "Their position is basically about consistency"; the overall (chief) animation director is "generally also the character designer" and "generally focus[es] on the faces of characters" — [Washi's Blog, anime production guide](https://washiblog.wordpress.com/2011/01/18/anime-production-detailed-guide-to-how-anime-is-made-and-the-talent-behind-it/)
- [practice][P] "The animation director's job is to make sure the characters in each scene match the character charts"; throughput is about three easy cuts a day, with hard cuts taking one to two days each — [ANN interview with Karin Omura](https://animenewsnetwork.com/interview/2024-11-08/what-exactly-does-an-animation-director-do-an-with-nokotan-karin-omura/.216976)
- [practice][P] Kyoto Animation character designers / chief animation directors roundtable — [Sakuga Blog translation](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2020/07/22/commanding-kyoanis-animation-character-designers-chief-supervisors-roundtable/):
  - Design starts from the director's intent and personality, not angles; typical director feedback is "This character would never make that expression."
  - Iteration: one designer went to the director "three or four times" with proposals; several redraw the whole design from scratch on a new sheet rather than patch it; one copies the sheet before redrawing because "Without the copy, there's no way to go back."
  - Sheets: "I start with all of the angles I want to draw, add on angles I'm missing after that"; sheets should show the character "from above, below, left, and right", and designers still wish they had drawn more angles; one sheet was assembled "like a collage" from earlier expression sheets.
  - Error prevention as a design goal: "I try to avoid elements in character sheets that invite such errors as much as I can" (the same designer uses very simple eyes).
  - Correction order as chief AD: outlines and eyes first, then the nose, after which changes spread: "It becomes hard on my end to gauge the feel of a drawing as a whole with just partial corrections." Full redraws are common.
  - Authority: "The chief animation director's corrections can be taken as the definitive answer."
  - Line colour is part of the design: coloured outlines for light hair and clothing patterns versus solid lines where depth is wanted.
- [practice][P] 斎藤敦史 (designer on a magical-girl and an idol franchise): "線が多いと原画担当がしんどい…できれば減らしたい" but "シンプルにしすぎると華やかさが無くなるし、パっと見た時の線の情報量が弱くなる"; designs are made "動かすことを意識し、動きの邪魔になるものは、極力排除"; the client asked to keep "キャラを印象付ける目と髪の情報量"; his method is to keep "ある程度の約束や決まり" while adding individuality — [Real Sound](https://realsound.jp/book/2024/02/post-1581975_2.html)
- [practice][P] Colour is a separate standardised track: the colour designer (色彩設定 / 色彩設計) is "the person establishing the show's overall palette" and supplies painters with the model sheets each episode requires — [Sakuga Blog glossary](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/glossary/)
- [practice][S] "On-model" means matching the character designer's intent; off-model animation can still be good animation — [Kanzenshuu](https://www.kanzenshuu.com/?p=66759). Corrections usually fix the expression and adjust some lines rather than redrawing the whole cut — [ANN credits guide](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/feature/2016-03-16/your-ultimate-guide-to-anime-ending-credits-part-i/.99852)
- [lore][P] Line economy conventions stated in instructional material: for a woman's face only the nose tip is indicated from the front; irises are vertically stretched; the classic eye-spacing rule "is often broken for a stylized effect" — [Clip Studio 描き方ナビ](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152504), [AnimeOutline](https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-face-structure/), [Tuts+](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-anime-heads-and-faces--cms-31884)
- [practice][P] In a 3D production that imitates anime, the head rig alone has about 170 bones, about 70 of them for facial expression, which indicates how much per-drawing facial adjustment the style demands — [4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140703095/)
- [lore][S, generic concept-art guides, not anime-studio testimony] Exploration by small thumbnails and solid-black silhouette tests, with hair mass and coat length named as the main silhouette carriers for anime characters — [Proko](https://www.proko.com/lesson/how-to-thumbnail-for-concept-art), [Unity Learn](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/thumbnails-designing-with-shape-and-silhouette)

### Inferences
- A settei is a set of *key views plus key expressions drawn by one hand*, and the on-model mechanism is a single person's judgement applied to every cut. The professional system assumes that a character cannot be specified by proportions and must be specified by exemplars. For a program, the analogue is: per-character key views and a single style authority (a designed part library or a house style), not a proportion vector.
- The correction order (outline, eyes, nose, then everything) is evidence about salience: the face outline and the eyes are where "off" is detected first. Those two parts deserve the highest design effort and the tightest constraints in a parametric system.
- The "line information" trade-off is a genuine second axis next to proportion: the same proportions read differently at different line densities, and designers tune it per audience and per production budget. It is partly parameterisable (number of lash strokes, hair strand lines, highlight count) as a "detail level" control.
- "Simple eyes invite fewer errors" generalises: simpler part designs are more robust to parameter variation. A parametric system should prefer part designs that stay valid across the whole slider range over ornate ones that only work at the default.

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source documenting what a professional eye-design sheet specifies (upper lash line, iris, pupil, highlight construction) or a documented convention for hair as large clumps; these remain uncited craft knowledge.
- 対比表 (height comparison charts) and 色指定 sheets are standard items in published settei collections, but I found no citable description of their contents in this pass.
- No anime-studio testimony was found for silhouette-first or thumbnail-first exploration; the only primary evidence on exploration is the Kyoto Animation roundtable (iterated full redraws and director review).
- Sakugabooru / settei-archive commentary on specific model sheets was not reviewed.

## 6. The "2D cheats" problem: drawings that are not 3D-consistent

### Takeaway
It is well documented, in both graphics research and production accounts, that cartoon and anime drawings of the same character from different angles do not correspond to any single 3D shape, and that productions which start from a 3D model must hand-edit normals, deform the mesh per shot or per frame, and sometimes hand-draw the face. A system that derives three-quarter and profile views by rotating one set of proportions will produce the "looks 3D / looks wrong" result that these productions spend their effort removing.

### Cited Findings
- [method][P, PDF text read] Rivers, Igarashi and Durand, "2.5D Cartoon Models" (SIGGRAPH 2010) — [paper PDF](https://www-ui.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~takeo/papers/alec_siggraph2010_cartoon.pdf):
  - "many stylistic elements of 2D drawings cannot be adequately reproduced in a 3D model, as the 2D appearance intended may not correspond to any real 3D shape."
  - "rendering from a single 3D model cannot account for cartoons that have mutually inconsistent appearances in different views – for example, no matter the viewing angle, Bugs Bunny's ears are always facing the camera."
  - Their alternative uses vector drawings of each part in a few key views, gives each stroke one 3D anchor for position and depth order, and interpolates the 2D shape; it "typically only requires three or four defined views". Strokes whose placement matches no 3D position can be left unanchored and interpolated purely in 2D; the mouth in their example is treated as a shape that "pops" between orientations.
  - Stated limitations: interior contour lines that should merge with the silhouette are unsupported; sharp or highly concave shapes interpolate poorly; "Hair is a difficult case for our system"; overlapping strokes pop; these "can only be ameliorated by drawing in more key views."
- [method][S] Rademacher, "View-Dependent Geometry" (SIGGRAPH 1999): a 3D model that changes shape with viewing direction, built from key deformations tied to reference drawings, because cel-animation drawings contain view-specific distortions — [NPR bibliography entry](https://isgwww.cs.uni-magdeburg.de/~stefans/npr/entry-Rademacher-1999-VDG.html)
- [method][P, abstract] Fukusato and Maejima (2021): "cartoon objects and characters have several distorted parts which do not correspond to any real 3D positions"; they formulate rotation of 2.5D models as a view-dependent deformation problem, blending user-specified 2D shapes of key views — [arXiv 2103.15472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15472)
- [method][S] Kitamura et al. (2015) start from the observation that illustrations of one character drawn from different views may be inconsistent — [paper PDF](https://kanamori.cs.tsukuba.ac.jp/publications/adada2015/kitamura_adada_2015_vol.18_no4.2.pdf)
- [practice][P] Guilty Gear Xrd (Arc System Works): "顔面や頭部では，アーティストが手作業で法線の編集調整を行っています" so that, for example, the cheek shades together with the temple; characters are about 40,000 polygons with about 460 to 600 bones; lighting position and angle are tuned "1コマ1コマ" in cut-scenes; battle and close-up models have different face proportions — [4Gamer, 西川善司](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140703095/)
- [practice][S, machine-generated timestamped summary of the GDC 2015 talk; the official handout PDF was unreachable] The guiding principle was "killing everything 3D"; automatic normals "often do not align with the artist's intentions"; animation is limited, with every frame treated as a keyframe and no interpolation because full animation "made the visuals look more 3D"; scale animation was added by hand to exaggerate; "mesh deformation at every keyframe introduces imperfections that prevent the model from appearing rigidly 3D"; some moves swap in separate meshes; shadows are painted via vertex data rather than computed — [video summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/yhGjCzxJV3E), [GDC Vault entry](https://gdcvault.com/play/1022031/GuiltyGearXrd-s-Art-Style-The)
- [practice][S] A fan summary attributes to the speaker: "If you find something that looks 3D, you just have to find a way to avoid it." — [noggi3d notes](https://noggi3d.notion.site/guilty-gear-style-summary)
- [practice][P] An indie cel-look production's rigger showed the face mesh being deformed for a profile shot of a talking character so the mouth reads as anime-like, and the top half of the face being scaled when the head tilts to suppress the depth of the eyeballs; the article's framing is "Faces get warped to create anime-like style" — [Anime News Network, 2022](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2022-03-02/indie-animation-staff-reveals-gnarly-cg-techniques-used-to-achieve-anime-look/.183151)
- [practice][P] Studio Orange used proprietary software to "simulate flat 2D expressions regardless of the angle they were shown from" and "employed countless hand-drawn corrections"; 2D animators drew pre-visualisation that was followed closely at emotional peaks — [Sakuga Blog on Beastars](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2019/12/04/beastars-traditionally-excellent-modernity/)
- [practice][S] On Land of the Lustrous the bodies were 3D but "the eyes and expressions weren't done in 3D - they were hand-drawn" — [ANN interview with the director](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interview/2020-05-30/beastars-director-shinichi-matsumi/.159723)
- [practice][S, practitioner write-ups] Genshin-style face shading does not use the face's geometric normals: breakdowns describe replacing them with a spherical proxy and driving the shadow boundary from an artist-authored map, made by drawing about nine shadow shapes for different light angles and baking them into one signed-distance texture — [stylised facial shadow workflow](https://sydianandrewchen.github.io/projects/3-stylized-facial-shadow-workflow), [face shadow map workflow](https://erichu33.notion.site/Face-Shadow-Map-Creation-Baking-Workflow-d3b8769021e04683a2f2ae4cf16ac810), [URP shader repo](https://github.com/NoiRC256/URPSimpleGenshinShaders)
- [method][S] A Japanese paper proposes exaggerating perspective in real-time 3D without "breaking" the character's face, on the premise that geometric correctness of 3D models produces a sense of wrongness relative to hand drawing — [CiNii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390298986213424512?lang=en)
- [practice][S] Artists publish tools that flatten an anime head toward the camera to fake an orthographic face at extreme angles — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/create-perfect-anime-faces-at-extreme-angles-with-this-blender-tool)

### Inferences
- For a 2D SVG system this is good news in one respect: it is already on the "2.5D" side of the divide, which is the side the research recommends. The Rivers et al. structure maps directly onto a parametric SVG creator: each part has a small number of key-view shapes (front, three-quarter, profile), a nominal anchor for placement and depth order, and 2D interpolation between views.
- The implication for the hypothesis is direct: proportions measured on front views do not determine the three-quarter or profile drawings. Each view needs its own measured or designed proportions (far-eye width and shape, cheek contour, mouth placement in profile), and some parts (mouth in profile, hair tufts, ears in some styles) should switch between designed shapes rather than be derived.
- Statistics gathered from a corpus must therefore be binned by view angle; pooling views would corrupt the proportion statistics, and frontal-only landmark detectors (28 points, near-frontal) will not supply the other bins.
- Hair is the documented hard case for view interpolation, which supports treating hair as a library of authored multi-view parts rather than as a parameterised shape.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source that enumerates the specific anime face cheats named in the brief (mouth drawn on the cheek in profile, far-eye shape, the "anime cheek" contour, hair tufts constant across angles) with measurements. Their existence is supported by the production accounts above but they are not quantified anywhere I could find.
- Nothing citable was found for Dragon Ball FighterZ specifically, and Studio Orange's angle-correction tool is described only at the level of one sentence.
- The official GDC handout (ggxrd.com PDF) could not be retrieved, so the per-frame scaling and deformation claims rest on a machine summary of the talk video; the hand-edited normals and model variants are confirmed by the 4Gamer primary article.

## 7. Synthesis: testing "proportions from competent drawings give good proportions for our characters"

### Takeaway
The hypothesis is true in a narrow sense and false in the sense that matters. Measured proportions reliably buy *category legibility* (anime rather than realistic, apparent age band, gender presentation, audience register), provided they are stratified by style, age, gender and view; they do not buy appeal, identity or cross-view correctness, which professionals control through eye and outline drawing, line density, hair masses, colour, and per-view authored shapes enforced by one person's judgement.

### Cited Findings
- [measured] Proportions carry category: eye opening 2.7x and eye area 3.4x human, nose 1.6x narrower — [Liu et al. 2019](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf); head and eye ratios track audience age at r = −0.72 and −0.67 — [Carter et al. 2016](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Designing-Animated-Characters-for-Children-of-Different-Ages-Paper.pdf); eye and lower-face ratios track character age — [Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022](https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1)
- [measured] Proportions under-determine the result: within-style SD of eye opening is half its mean — [Liu et al. 2019](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf); female character age is predicted within one year only 17% of the time from proportions — [Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022](https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1); averaging more female lead faces does not monotonically improve attractiveness or protagonist fit — [Ota 2024](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kaogaku/24/1/24_3/_article/-char/en); conventional proportions were not what child viewers preferred — [Carter et al. 2016](https://la.disneyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/Designing-Animated-Characters-for-Children-of-Different-Ages-Paper.pdf)
- [measured] The corpus is skewed: female-focused illustration data — [gwern.net](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021); rising moe-coded softness over time — [Liu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.05507)
- [method] Landmark geometry is low-dimensional and linear (32 components for 99%), but exaggeration is about direction, not scaled amplitude, and realistic bases do not extrapolate — [CariGANs](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.00222), [3D-CariGAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06841); PCA on appearance blurs — [RaBit](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Luo_RaBit_Parametric_Modeling_of_3D_Biped_Cartoon_Characters_With_a_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)
- [practice] Professionals specify by drawings and correct outlines and eyes first — [Kyoto Animation roundtable](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2020/07/22/commanding-kyoanis-animation-character-designers-chief-supervisors-roundtable/); identity is carried by "目と髪の情報量" and tuned against line count — [斎藤敦史 interview](https://realsound.jp/book/2024/02/post-1581975_2.html); eye shape variables map to temperament and age — [Mynavi excerpt](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/152284)
- [method/practice] Views are separately authored and mutually inconsistent; three or four key views per part suffice for plausible rotation, hair excepted — [Rivers et al. 2010](https://www-ui.is.s.u-tokyo.ac.jp/~takeo/papers/alec_siggraph2010_cartoon.pdf); 3D productions hand-edit normals and deform per shot — [4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/216/G021678/20140703095/), [ANN 2022](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/interest/2022-03-02/indie-animation-staff-reveals-gnarly-cg-techniques-used-to-achieve-anime-look/.183151)

### Inferences

**What proportions alone fix (given stratified statistics)**
- Reads as anime rather than realistic or Western cartoon (eye opening, nose width, lip-to-chin span, skull width).
- Apparent age band and audience register (head count, head-to-body ratio, eye height to head height, eye roundness, lower-face length).
- Gender presentation at body level (shoulder width relative to head, waist height, hip to chest ratio) and coarse build.
- Plausibility bounds: keeping sliders inside the observed range per style family prevents broken faces.

**What proportions do not fix**
- Which style family the character belongs to. A pooled mean lies between families and is weighted toward young, female, moe.
- Appeal and protagonist quality (the averaging and preference studies both fail to support "closer to the mean is better").
- Adult female age: the proportion signal is weak, so maturity must come from drawing choices.
- The drawing of each feature: lash line weight and shape, iris shape, pupil, highlight count and placement, nose mark, mouth line, line density, hair masses, colour.
- Three-quarter and profile views, which are separate designs with their own cheats.
- Whole-face harmony, which professionals judge holistically and enforce through one person.

**Remaining factors that are themselves parameterisable**
- Eye design as a small parameter set: opening height and width (roundness), outer-corner tilt (つり目 to たれ目), upper-lid droop, iris size relative to opening, iris vertical stretch, pupil size, highlight count, lash scheme (none / upper / lower / both) and lash weight. All of these are named, and mapped to age and temperament, in instructional sources.
- Face layout as recursive fractions: eye line on the head, eye spacing in eye-widths, nose and mouth as fractions of the eye-to-chin span, lower-face length, jaw width and chin squareness.
- An age axis and a "detail level" axis as *bundled* sliders that move several parameters together (age: eye size down, lower face up, head count up, iris and highlights down, lash drawing up; detail: lash strokes, hair strand lines, highlight count).
- Body: head count, shoulder width, waist height, crotch at half height, lower-leg lengthening.
- Per-view offsets: each parameter can carry separate values at front, three-quarter and profile key views with interpolation between them.

**Factors that need a library of designed parts**
- Hair (documented as the hard case for interpolation, and a primary identity carrier).
- Eye style families (the lash / iris / highlight drawing convention), with the parameters above acting within a family.
- Mouth shapes per view and expression, especially the profile mouth, which should switch rather than interpolate.
- Nose marks, ears, brows, and face-outline families per view.

**Factors that need human art direction**
- Choosing the style family and curating the corpus it is fitted on.
- Judging whole-face harmony and "would this character look like this", for which the professional mechanism is a single authoritative eye.
- Setting line density for the intended audience and rendering size.

**Practical consequences for the statistical plan**
- Stratify before averaging: by view angle, apparent age band, gender presentation and style family or era; expect the mature-adult strata to be thin and to need hand-set defaults from craft rules.
- Model as "discrete style centre plus small linear model" rather than one global mean; bound slider ranges by the stratum's observed spread.
- Seed defaults from numbers that already exist (section 2 table values, head-count presets from section 1) and reserve new measurement for what is missing: per-view ratios, eye-design parameters, and TV / illustration styles beyond the film sample.

### Gaps
