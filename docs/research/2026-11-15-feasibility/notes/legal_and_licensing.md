# Legal and licensing considerations: deriving statistics from copyrighted anime/manga drawings and publishing a parametric character generator (position as of 15 November 2026)

**This is background research, not legal advice.** It was compiled by a non-lawyer research agent from public sources. Anyone relying on it for a commercial release should have a qualified lawyer in their own jurisdiction check it.

**Provenance tags used throughout**

- **[P]** = read on a primary source this session (statute, judgment, official guidance, licence or terms page). The Agency for Cultural Affairs overview, the *DC Comics v. Towle* opinion and the US Copyright Office Part 2 report were read directly. Other [P] pages (EUR-Lex, LII, site terms) were read through an automated page reader that returns short verbatim quotes plus paraphrase, so wording outside the quotes is paraphrase.
- **[S]** = secondary source only (law-firm alert, trade press, commentary, or a search-result summary of such). The underlying judgment was not read.

## 1. Are proportions, ratios, measurements and aggregate statistics extracted from copyrighted works protectable?

### Takeaway
Settled in all three systems: copyright protects creative expression, not ideas, facts, methods or data, so corpus-level means, variances, ratios and principal components are not protectable subject matter and publishing them does not reproduce any work. The unsettled edge is not the aggregate statistics but (a) per-work or per-character measurement sets detailed enough to re-create a specific design, and (b) the copying needed to take the measurements (question 3).

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- [P] 17 U.S.C. §102(b): "In no case does copyright protection for an original work of authorship extend to any idea, procedure, process, system, method of operation, concept, principle, or discovery, regardless of the form in which it is described, explained, illustrated, or embodied in such work." — [Cornell LII, 17 U.S.C. §102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102)
- [P] *Feist Publications v. Rural Telephone*, 499 U.S. 340 (decided 27 March 1991): "[n]o author may copyright his ideas or the facts he narrates"; "The sine qua non of copyright is originality"; "the raw facts may be copied at will"; "copyright rewards originality, not effort"; the "sweat of the brow" doctrine was rejected. — [Cornell LII, Feist](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340)
- [S] *Mattel v. MGA Entertainment*, 616 F.3d 904 (9th Cir. 2010): the concept of "depicting a young, fashion-forward female with exaggerated features" is an "unprotectable idea"; "a finding of substantial similarity between two works can't be based on similarities in unprotectable elements". This is the closest US authority on exaggerated body proportions as an idea. — [University of Michigan Library guide quoting the opinion](https://guides.lib.umich.edu/substantial-similarity/mattel); background in [WIPO Magazine](https://www.wipo.int/wipo_magazine/en/2011/04/article_0006.html)
- [S] *Blehm v. Jacobs*, 702 F.3d 1193 (10th Cir. 2012): the stick-figure concept and common poses are unprotected; only the artist's "particular stylistic choices" in drawing his figures are protected, and the defendant's figures did not copy those. — [National Law Review summary](https://natlawreview.com/article/thin-copyrights-protected-not-infringed); opinion at [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca10/11-1479/11-1479-2012-12-27.html)

**European Union**
- [P] DSM Directive (EU) 2019/790, recital 9: "Text and data mining can also be carried out in relation to mere facts or data that are not protected by copyright", and in such cases no authorisation is required. — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [P] DSM Directive Art. 2(2) defines text and data mining as "any automated analytical technique aimed at analysing text and data in digital form in order to generate information which includes but is not limited to patterns, trends and correlations" — i.e. the directive itself treats the output of analysis as information, distinct from the works analysed. — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [P] CJEU *Painer* (C-145/10, 1 Dec 2011), paras 87-94: a subject matter is protected only if it is "original in the sense that it is its author's own intellectual creation", which requires that it "reflects the author's personality" through free and creative choices giving it a "personal touch". Protection therefore attaches to the creative choices, not to information about the work. — [EUR-Lex, C-145/10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62010CJ0145)
- [S] In *Like Company v Google* (C-250/25), Advocate General Szpunar's opinion of 3 Nov 2026 is reported as saying the press publishers' right does not protect the "mere facts" reported, only their form of expression (non-binding opinion; judgment pending). — [CJEU opinion page](https://curia.europa.eu/site/jcms/p1_1000088866/en/); [Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/eu-legal-adviser-finds-limited-uses-of-google-ai-could-run-afoul-of-publishers-rights/)

**Japan**
- [P] The Agency for Cultural Affairs' English overview of the "General Understanding on AI and Copyright in Japan" (Japan Copyright Office, May 2024) states that a work is a "creatively produced expression of thoughts or sentiments that falls within the literary, academic, artistic, or musical domain" and that "Copyright protection applies to the 'creative expressions' of an idea and not to the 'idea' itself." — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] Copyright Act Art. 30-4(ii), as translated in that overview, defines data analysis as "the extraction, comparison, classification, or other statistical analysis of the constituent language, sounds, images, or other elemental data from a large number of works or a large volume of other such data" — the statute itself names statistical analysis of images across many works. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [S] Japanese commentary on the Supreme Court's Popeye necktie judgment (17 July 1997) describes a "character" as an abstract concept sublimated from concrete expression and not itself a work; only the concrete drawings are works. — [Chambers / Ohebashi overview of character protection in Japan](https://chambers.com/articles/overview-of-character-protection-and-recent-cases-in-japan); [Keio SFC Journal article](https://gakkai.sfc.keio.ac.jp/journal/.assets/SFCJ-25-1-04.pdf)

### Inferences
- A statistic such as "mean eye height is X% of face height, standard deviation Y, across N drawings by many artists" is a fact about a population of works plus an idea (a proportion convention). It falls on the unprotected side under §102(b)/*Feist*, recital 9 of the DSM Directive, and the Japanese idea/expression rule. Head-to-body ratio is the same kind of thing as the "exaggerated features" held to be an idea in *Mattel v. MGA*.
- Principal components computed over many artists are directions of variation in the population, not the expression of any one work. That reasoning weakens as the corpus narrows: the Japanese guidance's point that a small group of works by one creator may share "common creative expressions", not merely a style (question 2), is the best available marker for where aggregation stops being safely abstract.
- Per-work measurement vectors are different in kind. A dense landmark set for one drawing is close to a tracing, and a parameter preset that makes the generator redraw one named character is a re-encoding of that character's design. [M] US courts have treated detailed "facts" about a fictional work as the author's protected expression rather than true facts (*Castle Rock Entertainment v. Carol Publishing*, 150 F.3d 132 (2d Cir. 1998), the Seinfeld trivia case). Publish aggregates only; treat per-image and per-character data as sensitive.
- [M] The EU sui generis database right (Directive 96/9/EC, Art. 7) protects against extraction of a substantial part of a database's contents, but Art. 11 limits it to makers who are EU nationals or residents, so it is unlikely to protect US- or Japan-based image boards. Not re-verified.

### Gaps
- No decision was found in any of the three jurisdictions that squarely addresses publishing measurements or statistics taken from visual artworks. The conclusion rests on general doctrine and analogy.
- Japan's Copyright Act Art. 2(1)(i) was read only as quoted in the Agency overview and a law-firm article, not on the official translation site.

## 2. Is art style protected, and where is the line between an "anime-style original character" and infringing a specific character's design?

### Takeaway
As a general rule style, genre conventions and technique are not protected in the US, EU or Japan, while a specific, sufficiently distinctive character design is. The line is whether the new character reproduces the particular creative expression of an identifiable existing character (not the shared conventions) and was derived from it; that general rule is settled, but its margins are actively contested (single-artist style imitation, trade-dress theories, and the US Copyright Office's "market dilution" theory).

### Cited Findings

**United States — characters**
- [P] *DC Comics v. Towle* (9th Cir., 23 Sept 2015) sets a three-part test: "First, the character must generally have 'physical as well as conceptual qualities.' ... Second, the character must be 'sufficiently delineated' to be recognizable as the same character whenever it appears. Considering the character as it has appeared in different productions, it must display consistent, identifiable character traits and attributes, although the character need not have a consistent appearance. ... Third, the character must be 'especially distinctive' and 'contain some unique elements of expression.' ... It cannot be a stock character such as a magician in standard magician garb." — [Ninth Circuit opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2015/09/23/13-55484.pdf)
- [P] The same opinion notes that comic-book characters are more readily protected than literary ones because a comic character "has physical as well as conceptual qualities" and "is more likely to contain some unique elements of expression", and that characters that are "lightly sketched" may not merit protection. It also discusses the older Warner Bros. v. CBS ("story being told") line as dicta or an alternative holding. — [Ninth Circuit opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2015/09/23/13-55484.pdf)

**United States — style, stock elements, scope**
- [S] *Mattel v. MGA*: where an idea admits many expressions, protection is broad and the test is substantial similarity; where the range of expression is narrow (the doll sculpt was "highly constrained"), protection is "thin" and only "virtually identical" copying infringes. For the sketches the court found "a wide range of expression for complete young, hip female fashion dolls with exaggerated features". — [University of Michigan Library guide](https://guides.lib.umich.edu/substantial-similarity/mattel)
- [S] *Andersen v. Stability AI* (N.D. Cal., order of 12 Aug 2024): the court stated "Plaintiffs have no protection over 'simple, cartoony drawings' or 'gritty fantasy paintings'", but allowed copyright claims and Lanham Act false-endorsement and trade-dress claims against Midjourney to survive the pleading stage. No merits ruling yet; summary-judgment hearing reported moved to 17 Feb 2027 and trial to spring 2027. — [IPWatchdog on the Aug 2024 order](https://ipwatchdog.com/2024/08/14/california-court-issues-mixed-order-pivotal-ai-copyright-case/); [scheduling report](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2026/02/03/sarah-andersens-copyright-lawsuit-the-1st-filed-v-ai-companies-gets-pushed-back-again-at-her-request/); [court scheduling order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.407208/gov.uscourts.cand.407208.409.0.pdf)
- [S] US commentary: courts have commonly rejected attempts to protect an artist's style, reasoning that copyright covers fixed expression rather than abstract ideas. — [Northwestern JTIP, Feb 2026](https://jtip.law.northwestern.edu/2026/02/06/when-style-becomes-substance/). A Harvard JOLT article argues that some elements called "style" can be copyrightable and that substantial similarity is where the line is drawn. — [Sobel, Harvard JOLT](https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/assets/articlePDFs/v38/2-Sobel.pdf). A search summary of a 2025 Duke article reported that the federal courts that have considered whether style is protected are divided; this was not verified against the article. — [Duke Law scholarship repository](https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/faculty_scholarship/4522)
- [S] The US Copyright Office's Part 3 report on generative-AI training (pre-publication version, May 2025) advanced a "market dilution" theory: AI output in the same style or kind may harm the market for the training works even without substantial similarity. Commentators call the theory novel; no final version had been confirmed as of mid-2026, and the report does not bind courts. — [Sidley summary](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/05/28/generative-ai-meets-copyright-scrutiny-highlights-from-the-copyright-offices-part-iii-report/); [Mondaq, July 2026 status](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/licensing-syndication/1812988/scotus-ruling-re-opens-the-door-on-the-copyright-offices-ai-training-report)
- [S] *Disney / Universal v. Midjourney* (C.D. Cal., filed June 2025, consolidated with Warner Bros.' suit) targets generation of specific copyrighted characters, not style. As of mid-2026 it was in discovery with no merits ruling. — [The Art Newspaper, July 2026](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/07/09/midjourney-demands-hollywood-AI-secrets); [Law360](https://law360.com/articles/2489948)

**Japan**
- [P] Agency for Cultural Affairs overview: "an AI-generated material which applies the 'creator's style' of a preexisting copyrighted work does not infringe copyright if the style merely encompasses an idea." It adds that the idea/expression line "has been determined on a case-by-case basis" and that "A small group of works consisting only of specific creator's copyrighted works may not only have a common 'creator's style' (i.e., idea), but may also include common 'creative expressions.'" — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] Infringement in Japan requires both "similarity (i.e., common creative expression) and dependence (i.e., creation based on existing copyrighted work)"; without either there is no infringement. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [S] A character's concept (name, personality, background) is an idea; only specific visual depictions are protected. In a case about a Chinese online game, infringement was found for one character that reproduced the plaintiff's creative expression and denied for another where only ideas were shared (for example, a seated woman in a light-blue top). — [Chambers / Ohebashi, Dec 2024](https://chambers.com/articles/overview-of-character-protection-and-recent-cases-in-japan)
- [S] Japanese IP commentary (July 2025): 画風/作風 are not legally defined terms and denote abstract ideas, atmosphere and technique below the level of concrete expression; similarity is judged after removing technique, motif, idea and commonplace expression. Re-drawing an existing character "in another style" while keeping its creative expression is likely infringing; reproducing only the atmosphere is not. The author is a writer with corporate IP experience, not identified as a lawyer, and cites no Japanese judgments. — [Bengoshi JP News (ben54.jp)](https://www.ben54.jp/news/2418)

**European Union**
- [P] *Painer*: protection depends on free and creative choices, and paras 97-99 hold that the scope of protection is not reduced for a category of work merely because creative freedom is narrower. — [EUR-Lex, C-145/10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62010CJ0145)

### Inferences
- Practical line, combining the authorities: genre-standard anime features (large eyes, small nose and mouth, simplified shading, common hair shapes, chibi or 7-8-head proportions, school uniforms) are ideas, stock elements or commonplace expression. What is protected is the particular combination that makes one character recognisable: hair silhouette plus colour scheme plus costume plus signature accessories or markings, drawn in a particular way. A generated character infringes when that combination is recognisably reproduced and the reproduction derives from the original.
- Because both US law (copying, shown by access plus similarity) and Japanese law (dependence) require derivation, independent creation is a defence in principle. Holding a character's images in the measurement corpus establishes access, so the defence is strongest when the pipeline provably never stores or uses per-character information.
- The Japanese "small group of works" reasoning, the US "market dilution" theory and the surviving trade-dress claim in *Andersen* all point the same way: imitating one identifiable artist is where "style" claims have traction. Averaging over many artists is the structural answer.
- [M] Further US authorities commonly cited for these propositions, not re-verified: *Nichols v. Universal Pictures* (2d Cir. 1930, "sufficiently delineated"); *Walt Disney v. Air Pirates* (9th Cir. 1978); *Daniels v. Walt Disney* (9th Cir. 2020, lightly sketched characters unprotected under *Towle*); *Steinberg v. Columbia Pictures* (S.D.N.Y. 1987, style as one ingredient of expression); *Dave Grossman Designs v. Bortin* (N.D. Ill. 1972, anyone may paint in a cubist manner); the US Copyright Office Part 1 report (July 2024), recalled as declining to recommend protection for artistic style as such.
- [M] EU authorities recalled but not re-verified: *Infopaq* (C-5/08, 2009: reproduction "in part" covers any element that expresses the author's own intellectual creation); *Levola Hengelo* (C-310/17, 2018: subject matter must be identifiable with precision and objectivity); *Mio/konektra* (C-580/23, Dec 2025: infringement turns on recognisable reproduction of creative elements, not overall impression). [M] Japan: the Supreme Court's *Esashi Oiwake* judgment (2001) on directly perceiving the essential features of the original's expression.

### Gaps
- No Japanese court judgment specifically holding that 画風 is unprotected was retrieved; the proposition rests on the Agency for Cultural Affairs document and commentary. The names of candidate cases (for example the "professor illustration" case) surfaced in search but were not verified.
- No CJEU ruling specifically on protection of fictional characters or of style was found; EU position is inferred from the general originality case law.
- The exact wording of *Mattel v. MGA* on "oversized heads and feet" was not confirmed against the opinion.

## 3. Is the act of downloading and analysing the images lawful?

### Takeaway
Japan gives the clearest permission (Art. 30-4 covers statistical analysis of images for any purpose, including commercial, subject to a proviso); the EU permits it for anyone under DSM Art. 4 only where access is lawful and the rightholder has not opted out in a machine-readable way, with copies deleted when no longer needed; the US has no statutory exception and relies on fair use, which is fact-specific and currently unsettled. Non-generative statistical measurement is a materially stronger case than model training on every factor the courts and agencies have used, but no court has ruled on it and the 2026 Third Circuit ruling shows "non-generative" is not a safe harbour on its own.

### Cited Findings

**Japan**
- [P] Art. 30-4 permits exploitation "in any way and to the extent considered necessary" where the purpose is not to enjoy the thoughts or sentiments expressed in the work, including "for use in data analysis"; "provided, however, that this does not apply if the action would unreasonably prejudice the interests of the copyright owner in light of the nature or purpose of the work or the circumstances of its exploitation". — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] The overview says non-enjoyment exploitation "such as AI development or other forms of data analysis may, in principle, be allowed without the permission of the copyright holder." The exception fails where an enjoyment purpose coexists, for example collecting works for fine-tuning aimed at generating material similar to the collected works, or intentionally reproducing a small group of one creator's works in order to output their common creative expressions. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] "The presence or absence of an 'enjoyment' purpose is determined by 'the work' exploited under the Article, not by other copyrighted works or not copyrighted elements such as an 'artist's style'." — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] Proviso: assessed by "whether it will compete in the market with the copyrighted work" and "whether it will impede the potential sales channels of the copyrighted work in the future". The stated example is reproducing a database work that is sold or licensed for information analysis; reproducing database works while circumventing technical measures such as robots.txt or ID/password authentication is said to fall within the proviso. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] "Collecting AI training data from websites while being aware that they distribute pirated or copyright-infringing content should be strictly avoided." — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] Status: the General Understanding (Legal Subcommittee of the Cultural Council; overview dated May 2024) "is not legally binding" and is the subcommittee's "tentative consensus", noting that there are "very few court precedents" on AI and copyright. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf); Japanese materials at [文化庁 AIと著作権](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/chosakuken/aiandcopyright.html)
- [S] No Japanese court ruling on Art. 30-4 was found. The first major AI suits (Yomiuri, then Asahi and Nikkei, v. Perplexity, Tokyo District Court, filed Aug 2025) concern reproduction of articles in AI search and were at an early stage in May 2026. — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/08/japans-largest-newspaper-yomiuri-shimbun-sues-perplexity-for-copyright-violations/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/08/26/perplexity_asahi_nikkei_lawsuits/)

**European Union**
- [P] DSM Art. 3: mandatory exception for "research organisations and cultural heritage institutions" mining for "scientific research" works "to which they have lawful access"; copies may be retained for research purposes including verification. — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [P] DSM Art. 4: exception for "reproductions and extractions of lawfully accessible works and other subject matter for the purposes of text and data mining", open to anyone; copies "may be retained for as long as is necessary for the purposes of text and data mining"; it applies only where use "has not been expressly reserved by their rightholders in an appropriate manner, such as machine-readable means in the case of content made publicly available online." — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [P] Recital 18: for content publicly available online, reservation "should only be considered appropriate" if made "by the use of machine-readable means", which can include "metadata and terms and conditions of a website or a service". Art. 7(1): "Any contractual provision contrary to the exceptions provided for in Articles 3, 5 and 6 shall be unenforceable" — Art. 4 is not in that list, so contract can override it. — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [P] EU AI Act Art. 53(1)(c)-(d) obliges providers of general-purpose AI models to "put in place a policy to comply with Union law on copyright and related rights", in particular to identify and comply with rights reservations under DSM Art. 4(3), and to publish "a sufficiently detailed summary about the content used for training"; applicable from 2 Aug 2025. — [AI Act Art. 53 (annotated text)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/53/)
- [S] *Kneschke v. LAION*: Hamburg Regional Court (27 Sept 2024) held dataset creation covered by the scientific-research exception; the Hamburg Higher Regional Court (10 Dec 2025, 5 U 104/24) dismissed the appeal, applied both the scientific and the general TDM exceptions, and held that a natural-language reservation did not meet the machine-readability requirement as of 2021. A further appeal to the Federal Court of Justice (I ZR 281/25) is pending; one source gives 17 Dec 2026 as a decision date (unconfirmed). — [Technology's Legal Edge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2025/12/robert-kneschke-v-laion-judgment-of-10-december-2025-ref-5-u-104-24/); [Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102mebl/ai-training-copyright-part-2-text-and-data-mining-under-court-scrutiny); [Open Future](https://openfuture.eu/blog/laion-round-2-machine-readable-but-still-not-actionable/)
- [S] *GEMA v. OpenAI* (Munich Regional Court I, 11 Nov 2025, 42 O 14139/24): song lyrics memorised in model parameters are reproductions; the TDM exception may cover analysis of training data but not lasting storage of the works in the model. First instance, not final. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/deu/legal-updates/gema-vs.-openai-munich-regional-court-i-issues-landmark-copyright-decision)
- [S] *Like Company v Google* (C-250/25): AG opinion 3 Nov 2026 advises that the two questions on LLM training are inadmissible on the facts; the CJEU has not yet ruled, so there is still no CJEU judgment on TDM and AI. — [CJEU opinion page](https://curia.europa.eu/site/jcms/p1_1000088866/en/)

**United States**
- [S] *Authors Guild v. Google* (2d Cir., 16 Oct 2015): wholesale scanning of books to provide search and snippets was transformative fair use because it provided information about the books without substituting for them. — [US Copyright Office fair-use index summary](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/authorsguild-google-2dcir2015.pdf)
- [S] *Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence*: D. Del. (Bibas J., 11 Feb 2025) rejected fair use for training a non-generative legal-search tool on Westlaw headnotes; the Third Circuit affirmed on 29 Sept 2026, described as "the first federal appellate decision to address fair use in AI training". Factors one, three and four went against Ross (commercial, "minimally transformative", a competing product, and harm to a "rapidly developing" market for licensing AI training data); factor two slightly favoured Ross. The court rejected the intermediate-copying analogy: "ROSS does not need to copy Thomson Reuters's headnotes to access the underlying unprotected information" and "ease is not a justification for copying". It stressed that Ross's tool "cannot generate original expression" and did not decide generative-AI training. The opinion was issued under seal pending redactions. — [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved); [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/what-thomson-reuters-v-ross-does-and-doesnt-say-now-about-fair-use-and-generative-ai/); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/09/30/third-circuit-affirms-revised-fair-use-ruling-against-ross-ai-legal-research-platform-in-sealed-opinion/)
- [S] *Bartz v. Anthropic* (N.D. Cal., Alsup J., 23 June 2025): training an LLM on books was "exceedingly transformative" fair use, as was scanning lawfully purchased books; keeping a library of pirated copies was not. The class settlement of $1.5 billion received final approval on 20 July 2026 and releases only past conduct, not output claims. — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/intellectual-property/publications/ai-and-the-fair-use-defense-lessons-from-two-recent-summary-judgment-rulings/); [Authors Guild on final approval](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)
- [S] *Kadrey v. Meta* (N.D. Cal., Chhabria J., 25 June 2025): summary judgment of fair use for Meta on the record, with the judge stressing market harm as the key factor and warning that in many cases unlicensed training will be unlawful. In March 2026 plaintiffs were allowed to add contributory-infringement allegations about BitTorrent; in June 2026 they sought leave for an interlocutory appeal. No Ninth Circuit ruling found. — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/intellectual-property/publications/ai-and-the-fair-use-defense-lessons-from-two-recent-summary-judgment-rulings/); [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/ai-vs-authors-update-court-approves-historic-anthropic-settlement-while-meta-litigation-continues); [Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2487597)
- [S] *Getty Images v Stability AI* (England and Wales High Court, [2025] EWHC 2863 (Ch), 4 Nov 2025; UK, not EU): the Stable Diffusion model weights do not store or reproduce the images and so are not an "infringing copy"; secondary-infringement claim rejected; primary infringement and database claims had been dropped. Permission to appeal granted Dec 2025; appeal pending as CA-2026-000007. — [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/getty-images-v-stability-ai-uk-high-court-finds-no-secondary-copyright-infringement-in-landmark-genai-ruling/); [Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102lydc/getty-images-v-stability-ai-getty-granted-permission-to-appeal); [appellant's skeleton, Nov 2026](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Appellants-Replacement-Appeal-Skeleton--CA-2026-000007-144861071_1.pdf)

### Inferences
- **How measurement compares with model training.**
  - Output: a trained generative model can emit protected expression (memorisation was decisive in *GEMA*, and absent in *Getty*). Aggregate statistics cannot contain any work, so the output-side infringement theories do not apply to the published repository.
  - Japan: "statistical analysis of ... images ... from a large number of works" is the textbook Art. 30-4(ii) case; there is no generation purpose that could import an "enjoyment" purpose, provided the corpus is not a single creator's works used to recreate their expression.
  - EU: measurement is squarely "text and data mining" under Art. 2(2). The live constraints are lawful access, machine-readable opt-outs, and deleting copies once the analysis is finished. The AI Act duties attach to providers of general-purpose AI models; a deterministic parametric SVG generator is very unlikely to be one, so the Act matters mainly as confirmation that Art. 4(3) opt-outs are the operative EU mechanism.
  - US: the only prima facie infringing act is making copies (downloading). Purpose (extracting unprotected information), non-substitution and absence of expressive output favour fair use in the way *Authors Guild v. Google* did. Against that, the Third Circuit's reasoning (commercial purpose, strict "necessity" for intermediate copying, an emerging data-licensing market) could be turned on any commercial tool builder, although Ross's tool directly competed with the plaintiff and used its editorial content after a licence was refused. The Third Circuit binds only its own circuit.
- A pipeline that fetches an image, computes landmarks in memory and discards the image minimises the reproduction that all three systems regulate, and answers the Third Circuit's "necessity" point better than keeping a permanent mirror. [M] In the EU, on-screen and cache copies made while browsing were held covered by the temporary-copy exception in *Public Relations Consultants Association v NLA* (C-360/13, 2014); not re-verified.
- Source legality matters separately from use: *Bartz* (pirated library not fair use) and the Japanese guidance (knowingly collecting from sites with infringing content) both treat acquisition from infringing sources as an aggravating factor. Image boards that host reposts without artists' permission are exposed to that argument.
- [M] Copying is generally judged under the law of the place where it happens, so the developer's location when downloading largely determines which of these regimes governs the corpus-building step; the published repository contains no copies and is judged on its own content. Not researched.

### Gaps
- The unsealed Third Circuit opinion was not available to read; panel, precedential status and any rehearing petition are unknown.
- No US, EU or Japanese decision addresses non-generative measurement of images to derive design statistics. Whether an image board hosting unauthorised reposts counts as "lawfully accessible" (EU) or as a site known to distribute infringing content (Japan) is unlitigated.
- Whether Japanese law lets site terms override Art. 30-4 was not researched. UK law (no commercial TDM exception, as recalled) was out of scope and not verified.
- Other 2026 US rulings may exist that the searches did not surface (for example in the OpenAI multidistrict litigation or the music cases).

## 4. Dataset-specific issues and cleaner sources

### Takeaway
The popular anime corpora are the legally weakest inputs: Danbooru-derived datasets consist of third-party uploads whose copyright stays with the artists, pixiv's terms restrict crawling and prejudicial data analysis, and full Manga109 is restricted to academic use by non-commercial organisations. Manga109-s is the notable exception: its terms expressly allow commercial use of results obtained from image-processing experiments, subject to an approved application and attribution.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Manga109: "The dataset can be used solely for academic purposes", by non-commercial organisations; "Redistribution of any part of the dataset to third parties is forbidden." The 109 volumes were authorised for research use by their authors. — [Manga109 project site](https://manga109.github.io/manga109-project-website/en/); gated access and the academic/non-academic table at [Hugging Face, Manga109](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hal-utokyo/Manga109)
- [P] Manga109-s (87 of the 109 volumes) is "available for commercial use" under conditions. Permitted: using the dataset for machine-learning or image-processing experiments, and using "results, or portions of results, obtained from machine learning experiments" or image-processing experiments commercially. Conditions: an application describing the intended use; "the use of the Manga109-s dataset must be indicated clearly within the published work"; "Redistribution of the Manga109-s dataset to third parties is forbidden"; selling images together with results is forbidden; copies or modifications of the images "must not be treated as products". — [Hugging Face, Manga109-s](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hal-utokyo/Manga109-s)
- [P] Danbooru terms of service (shown as last updated 23 Oct 2022): content "is the sole responsibility of the person who submitted the Content"; uploaders warrant they hold the necessary rights and grant Danbooru a licence; rightsholders can use a DMCA process; API users must respect documented rate limits; bots or scripts "that cause harm to the Site" are prohibited. The terms as read do not mention datasets, commercial reuse or AI training. — [Danbooru terms of service](https://danbooru.donmai.us/terms_of_service)
- [P] The maintainer's page for the Danbooru2021 dataset says the site's founder and his successor gave permission for scraping; the release "has been taken offline"; and it is uncertain whether releases will resume, citing among other things "the changed legal climate". A note in the page source says "it is impossible to assure that every single image is legal to possess." The page's metadata lists CC0, but the page does not say that this covers the images or address the artists' copyright. — [Gwern, Danbooru2021](https://gwern.net/danbooru2021)
- [S] Later Danbooru-derived releases on Hugging Face carry licence files written by the dataset uploader (for example a modified MIT licence with an indemnification clause), not by Danbooru or the artists. — [deepghs/danbooru2024 LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/datasets/deepghs/danbooru2024/blob/main/LICENSE)
- [P] pixiv Master Terms of Use (latest listed revision 22 June 2026, revising Art. 14 among others), Art. 14 prohibits: reproducing posted works without the rightholder's consent (item 2); activities with a commercial or profit-making objective using the service (item 3); conduct that unreasonably prejudices posting users "by using the results of data analysis (including the conduct of learning to develop artificial intelligence" unless the posting user permitted the analysis (item 4); excessive server load and mechanically repeated actions (items 23-24). pixiv's guidelines also prohibit "Use of crawlers or other programs to aggregate works". The English text is a reference translation; the Japanese prevails. — [pixiv terms and guidelines](https://policies.pixiv.net/en.html)
- [S] US scraping and terms of service: in *Meta v. Bright Data* (N.D. Cal., 23 Jan 2024) the court held Meta's terms did not reach logged-out scraping of public data; in *X Corp. v. Bright Data* (N.D. Cal., 9 May 2024) state-law claims based on scraping and selling public data were held pre-empted by the Copyright Act. Both are district-court rulings. — [Farella Braun + Martel on Meta v. Bright Data](https://www.fbm.com/data-analytics/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [Frankfurt Kurnit on X Corp. v. Bright Data](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102j7s5/blockbuster-ruling-federal-court-holds-that-copyright-act-preempts-xs-web-scrap)
- [P] EU: website terms and conditions are named in recital 18 as a possible vehicle for an Art. 4(3) reservation, and Art. 7(1) does not protect Art. 4 against contractual override. — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790). [S] But the Hamburg appeal court held a natural-language reservation insufficiently machine-readable on the facts of 2021. — [Technology's Legal Edge](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2025/12/robert-kneschke-v-laion-judgment-of-10-december-2025-ref-5-u-104-24/)
- **Cleaner sources verified this session**
  - [P] MakeHuman: bundled assets (base mesh, proxies, targets and modifiers) "have been released under CC0 1.0 Universal"; exports are regarded "as your data, which is yours to handle as you see fit"; third-party community assets carry their own licences. — [MakeHuman LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/master/LICENSE.md)
  - [S] VRoid: sample models from the alpha versions of VRoid Studio were released by VRoid under CC0, whereas current base models and presets are not CC0 (see question 6). — [OpenGameArt listing of VRoid CC0 models](https://opengameart.org/content/vroid-studio-cc0-models); [VRoid Studio Guidelines](https://vroid.com/en/studio/guidelines)
  - [S] Japan extended the general term from 50 to 70 years after the author's death on 30 Dec 2018 without reviving works already in the public domain; many pre-1953 films are treated as public domain, with complications. — [Tokyo National Research Institute for Cultural Properties](https://www.tobunken.go.jp/materials/nenshi-en/2388106.html); [InfoDocket](https://www.infodocket.com/2018/12/10/japan-to-extend-copyright-period-on-works-including-novels-and-paintings-to-70-years-on-dec-30/)
  - [P] Purely AI-generated material is not protected by US copyright ("Copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material"). — [US Copyright Office, Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Academic-only data for a commercial tool.** A solo developer is not a "non-commercial organisation" using the data "solely for academic purposes", so full Manga109 is unavailable on its face. Publishing statistics derived from it for a commercial tool would breach the licence even though the statistics themselves are not copyrightable: the licence is a contract accepted to obtain gated access, and loss of the licence also removes the permission for the copies made. In the EU, Art. 4 is contractually overridable. [M] In the US, clickwrap terms on gated data are generally enforceable (*ProCD v. Zeidenberg*, 7th Cir. 1996), unlike terms on public pages that the scraper never accepted; not re-verified.
- **Manga109-s fits the use case.** "Results obtained from image processing experiments" describes landmark statistics well. The obligations are an honest application, a clear acknowledgement in the repository, credit lines where images are shown, and never redistributing or shipping the images. It is 87 volumes of professional manga from the 1970s-2010s, so it is narrow in era and medium (monochrome manga, not colour illustration or anime).
- **Danbooru-derived data.** The dataset licences on hosting sites cannot grant rights in the underlying images. Risk comes from the artists' copyright in the downloads (question 3) and from the unauthorised-source argument, not from Danbooru's own terms, which as read do not forbid analysis. Site-owner permission to scrape is not the artists' permission.
- **pixiv.** Crawling breaches the terms, and in the EU the terms are a plausible (contested) rights reservation. Item 4 targets prejudicial use of analysis results, which aggregate proportions are unlikely to be, but the crawling ban applies regardless.
- **Ranking of sources, cleanest first (inference):** own drawings and commissioned drawings with a written assignment or licence covering analysis; CC0 material (MakeHuman, the VRoid alpha samples) and CC-licensed art within its licence terms; Manga109-s with approval; proportion conventions taught in drawing manuals (the convention is an idea or method under §102(b); the manual's own illustrations and text remain protected) [M: *Baker v. Selden*, 101 U.S. 99 (1880), not re-verified]; genuinely public-domain works; then scraped boards and pixiv last. Renders of permissively licensed 3D models are clean but measure the model maker's proportions, not the hand-drawn corpus.
- AI-generated anime images are a tempting "copyright-free" corpus in the US, but they inherit whatever style concentration the generating model has, may be subject to the generating service's terms, and are not clearly unprotected in Japan or the EU when a human directed them (question 7).

### Gaps
- Terms for Safebooru, Gelbooru, Sankaku and for individual Hugging Face dataset cards were not read. Danbooru's robots.txt and API documentation were not checked.
- The full Manga109 licence text (beyond the project page and dataset card) was not read; whether the application review would approve this specific use is unknown.
- No verified list of CC-licensed anime-style illustration collections was compiled; official "free to use" material from publishers or studios was not surveyed.

## 5. Part libraries: copying a specific character's protectable expression; trademark and unfair-competition angles

### Takeaway
The part library and any presets are the main infringement exposure of the project, because they are the only place where specific expression is authored. A commonplace feature drawn afresh is safe; a closely copied distinctive rendering, or a set of parts that reassembles one character, is not, and how distinctive a single feature must be has no bright-line rule. Character names and well-known designs are separately protected by trademark and unfair-competition law in both the US and Japan.

### Cited Findings
- [P] *Towle*: protection requires a character to be "especially distinctive" and to "contain some unique elements of expression"; stock characters are excluded. — [Ninth Circuit opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2015/09/23/13-55484.pdf)
- [S] *Mattel v. MGA*: where expression is highly constrained (a doll sculpt), only "virtually identical" copying infringes; where the range of expression is wide (full character sketches), ordinary substantial similarity applies. — [University of Michigan Library guide](https://guides.lib.umich.edu/substantial-similarity/mattel)
- [S] *Blehm v. Jacobs*: the plaintiff held copyright in "particular stylistic choices" (for example a specific oversized half-moon smile on a black face), but shared ideas, poses and the figure concept were free, and differences in how the smile was drawn defeated the claim. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/thin-copyrights-protected-not-infringed)
- [S] *Andersen* (12 Aug 2024): a Lanham Act trade-dress claim over imitation of artists' recognisable look survived a motion to dismiss against Midjourney, in a context where artists' names were used as prompts; the court also said there is no protection over generic looks such as "simple, cartoony drawings". — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2024/08/14/california-court-issues-mixed-order-pivotal-ai-copyright-case/)
- [S] US trademark in characters is real but bounded and unsettled: in *Fleischer Studios v. A.V.E.L.A.* the Ninth Circuit first barred trademark claims over Betty Boop's image on aesthetic-functionality and *Dastar* grounds (Feb 2011), then withdrew that opinion and issued a replacement (654 F.3d 958, Aug 2011); secondary sources disagree on how the replacement disposed of the image-mark and word-mark claims. — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2011/08/fleischer-studios-inc-v-avela-inc); [Tushnet's 43(B)log](https://tushnet.blogspot.com/2011/02/betty-boop-as-material-girl.html)
- [S] Japan: characters are protected through several statutes rather than a single "merchandising right". Copyright protects specific visual depictions. The Trademark Act protects registered marks only (Art. 37 on similar marks and goods); character names and registered character designs can be marks (Pikachu is given as registered for goods including clothing and soft drinks). The Unfair Competition Prevention Act covers confusion with a well-known indication (Art. 2(1)(i)), use of a famous indication (Art. 2(1)(ii)) and imitation of product form (Art. 2(1)(iii)); once a character is well known, both its name and design can be protected. A character name is a symbol, not a work (the Dragon Quest "Luca" case, IP High Court). — [Chambers / Ohebashi, Dec 2024](https://chambers.com/articles/overview-of-character-protection-and-recent-cases-in-japan)
- [P] Picrew's guidelines prohibit "Contents created by tracing the work of other Users of the Service or third parties". — [Picrew Guidelines](https://support.picrew.me/en/picrewguidelines)

### Inferences
- **How distinctive must one feature be?** No authority found sets a threshold. Reading *Towle*, *Mattel* and *Blehm* together: a feature type (twin tails, an ahoge, cat-ear headband, heterochromia, a tsurime eye shape) is an idea or stock element; an original rendering of it by the part author is that author's own expression. Risk arises when a part reproduces the specific drawn form of a signature element that identifies one character (a unique hair ornament, a distinctive pupil or highlight motif, a costume emblem), or when several ordinary parts are bundled so that selecting them together yields the character. Thin-protection logic means near-identical copying of even simple shapes can infringe.
- **Dependence is created by workflow.** A part drawn while looking at one character's reference sheet is derived from it; a part drawn from the aggregate statistics and the artist's own design is not. Keep authorship records (who drew it, from what references).
- **Names are the cheapest risk to remove.** Part identifiers, preset names, file names and example galleries that use character or franchise names add trademark and unfair-competition exposure, signal derivation, and under the US case law make a false-endorsement or trade-dress claim easier to plead. Use descriptive, generic names.
- **Trademark and unfair-competition claims do not need copying of expression**, only a confusing or dilutive use of a well-known indication in trade. For a commercial card game or RPG, a character that consumers would take for a famous one is a problem even if every part was independently drawn.
- [M] *Mattel v. Goldberger Doll* (2d Cir. 2004) is recalled as holding that standard doll facial features are unprotected as ideas but a particularised rendering of them is protectable. [M] Japan's Supreme Court in *Gallop Racer* (2004) is recalled as refusing publicity-type rights in things or non-persons. Neither was re-verified.

### Gaps
- No case was found on infringement by a single reusable feature (one eye design or one hairstyle) in a character-creator or asset-pack context.
- Design rights (registered and unregistered EU designs; Japan's Design Act) and how they apply to character graphics were not researched.
- The conflicting accounts of the final *Fleischer* disposition were not resolved against the opinion.

## 6. Using third-party tools' outputs as a base (VRoid/VRM, Picrew/Charat, MakeHuman)

### Takeaway
MakeHuman is unambiguously safe to measure and to incorporate (CC0 assets and output). VRoid Studio output is commercially usable under pixiv's guidelines but its presets remain pixiv's property under licence, and each VRM file carries its own author-set permissions. Picrew is unsuitable: rights sit with each maker's creator, default scopes are narrow, and the guidelines restrict tracing and AI-related data analysis.

### Cited Findings
- [P] MakeHuman: source code under AGPL; assets "released under CC0 1.0 Universal" with "the intention of the MakeHuman project that anyone can do whatever they want with it"; "no output from MakeHuman contains any trace of program logic"; "As the assets have been released under CC0, there is no limitation on what you can do with this combined output"; for third-party assets "it is your own responsibility to make sure you abide by its specific license." — [MakeHuman LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/master/LICENSE.md)
- [S] VRoid Studio guidelines (official page, read only through a search summary because direct access was refused): model and clothing meshes and other preset items are content provided by pixiv, which retains the rights and licenses them broadly; anyone may sell or commercially use meshes, textures and preset items, and data made by modifying presets, unless a special clause applies; base models are not CC0; data from other creators follows that creator's terms; users set their own terms for models they create. The guidelines refer to Arts. 5 and 11 of the VRoid Studio Terms of Use, which were not read. — [VRoid Studio Guidelines](https://vroid.com/en/studio/guidelines); [VRoid Hub conditions of use for developers](https://developer.vroid.com/en/guidelines/conditions_of_use.html)
- [P] VRM licence metadata, set per model: who may operate the avatar (creator only, named persons, anyone); whether violent or sexual uses are allowed; whether commercial use is allowed; and a redistribution/modification licence type chosen from Redistribution Prohibited, CC0, CC BY, CC BY NC, CC BY SA, CC BY NC SA, CC BY ND, CC BY NC ND or Other, with an optional licence URL. — [VRM documentation, licence settings](https://vrm.dev/en/vrm/meta/license/)
- [P] Picrew guidelines: unless the creator explicitly permits, users may not perform "data analysis of images created using the Service based on generative AI" or use such images "as the prompt"; tracing others' work is prohibited; derivative works must keep a watermark or signature indicating they are derivative. — [Picrew Guidelines](https://support.picrew.me/en/picrewguidelines)
- [S] Picrew creators choose the permitted scope for each image maker (personal, non-commercial, commercial, processing); any use to obtain direct or indirect monetary benefit is commercial; a per-maker description takes precedence over the general examples. — [Picrew help: Setting the Scope of Image Use](https://support.picrew.me/en/?p=3017); [Picrew FAQ on derivative works](https://support.picrew.me/en/?p=1364)

### Inferences
- **Measure versus incorporate.** Measuring proportions of a model you lawfully made or obtained produces unprotected facts (question 1). Incorporating meshes, textures or part artwork into the repository is redistribution and needs a licence that permits it. Only CC0 material (MakeHuman assets, CC0-flagged VRM files, the VRoid alpha samples) can be put into an open-source repository without attribution or compatibility analysis.
- **VRoid as a measurement rig is reasonable.** Models a user builds in VRoid Studio encode pixiv's preset proportions, so measuring many of them samples one company's design, not the anime corpus. Read the VRM metadata of any third-party model before use; "Redistribution Prohibited" or non-commercial flags bar incorporation, though they do not obviously bar measurement.
- **Picrew.** The AI-analysis clause is worded around generative AI, so non-generative measurement is arguably outside it, but per-maker terms govern, most makers are personal or non-commercial, and each maker is one artist's style — the profile to avoid under question 2.
- MakeHuman's licence wording is a good template for the project's own licence: state explicitly that assets are CC0 (or otherwise) and that output belongs to the user without restriction.

### Gaps
- Charat's terms were not researched. The VRoid Studio Terms of Use text and any clause on analysis or extraction of preset data were not read. The current official VRoid page listing which sample models are CC0 was not reached; the CC0 status of the alpha samples rests on third-party pages.

## 7. Copyrightability of the generator's output

### Takeaway
A character composed by a human choosing part types and slider values in a deterministic tool is, in principle, a human-authored work in all three systems, though protection for the bare selection may be thin; nothing found addresses parametric (non-AI) generators specifically. If an AI agent chooses the parameters from a short instruction, the human's claim to that selection weakens sharply in the US and Japan, and what remains protectable is the human-drawn part artwork, human-made selection and arrangement, and later human modifications.

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- [P] US Copyright Office, *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability* (29 Jan 2025), conclusions: "The use of AI tools to assist rather than stand in for human creativity does not affect the availability of copyright protection for the output"; "Copyright protects the original expression in a work created by a human author, even if the work also includes AI-generated material"; "Copyright does not extend to purely AI-generated material, or material where there is insufficient human control over the expressive elements"; sufficiency "must be analyzed on a case-by-case basis"; "prompts do not alone provide sufficient control"; human authors are entitled to copyright in their works "perceptible in AI-generated outputs, as well as the creative selection, coordination, or arrangement of material in the outputs, or creative modifications of the outputs." — [Part 2 report (PDF)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf); announcement at [copyright.gov NewsNet 1060](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)
- [P] Same report: authors "have used computer-assisted technology for decades"; the assistive/generative distinction "depends on how the system is being used, not on its inherent characteristics"; "The issue is the degree of human control, rather than the predictability of the outcome"; and "authorship by adoption does not in itself provide a basis for claiming copyright in AI-generated outputs." A footnote notes that selection and arrangement of only two or three elements is generally insufficient. — [Part 2 report (PDF)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)
- [S] *Thaler v. Perlmutter*: the D.C. Circuit (2025) upheld the human-authorship requirement and the Supreme Court denied certiorari on 2 March 2026 (No. 25-449). A denial is not a merits ruling. *Allen v. Perlmutter* (D. Colo.), challenging refusal to register a Midjourney image made with more than 600 prompts, was still pending in March 2026 sources. — [SCOTUSblog case file](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/case-files/thaler-v-perlmutter); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)
- [S] Tool-maker versus user: the Ninth Circuit in *Design Data v. Unigate* (2017) acknowledged authority suggesting a program's copyright might extend to its output only if the program "does the lion's share of the work" and the user's role is "marginal" (from *Torah Soft v. Drosnin*, S.D.N.Y. 2001), and held a CAD vendor did not own users' output; *Rearden v. Disney* (N.D. Cal. 2018) dismissed a motion-capture software owner's claim to film output on that basis. — [Harvard JOLT Digest on Rearden](https://jolt.law.harvard.edu/digest/rearden-v-walt-disney-co-district-court-rejects-puzzling-copyright-allegation-but-permits-patent-and-trademark-claims-to-move-forward); [Foley & Lardner](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2018/08/when-can-a-company-be-liable-for-its-vendors-copyr/); [Loeb & Loeb on the 2023 ruling](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2023/03/rearden-llc-v-twdc-enterprises-18-corp)

**Japan**
- [P] "Materials autonomously generated by AI" (no instructions, or only simple ones such as "Draw a cat") are not works. If AI "is used as a 'tool' by a person to creatively express thoughts or sentiments, such material is considered a 'work', and the user of the AI the 'author'", depending on "creative intention" and "creative contribution". Factors: detailed instructions specifying creative expression count, long prompts that merely suggest ideas do not; many attempts alone do not, iterative checking and correction may; mere selection among outputs does not by itself; human additions and corrections that are creative expression are protected. Only a natural or legal person can be an author. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [S] On 20 Nov 2025 Chiba prefectural police referred a man to prosecutors for copying a Stable Diffusion image onto a book cover, treating the image as a work because it was made through repeated specific instructions; reported as the first such enforcement in Japan. This is a police referral, not a court ruling. — [Kumamoto Nichinichi Shimbun (Kyodo)](https://kumanichi.com/articles/1927176)

**European Union**
- [P] The originality test is the author's own intellectual creation reflecting free and creative choices (*Painer*). — [EUR-Lex, C-145/10](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:62010CJ0145)
- [S] Commentary (2026): EU law rests on human authorship; fully autonomous AI output is outside protection; AI-assisted output may be protected where a human keeps creative control (selecting, iterating, editing, combining), while generic prompts alone are insufficient. Italy legislated in 2025 that works made with AI tools are protected if they result from human intellectual effort; a 2026 German first-instance decision on AI-generated logos contemplated protection through human intervention; a December 2025 European Parliamentary Research Service study rejected a sui generis right. No CJEU ruling yet. — [DLA Piper, April 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-la/insights/publications/2026/04/ai-and-copyright-major-issues-and-direction-of-travel-in-the-uk-eu-and-china-in-q2-2026/can-ai-generated-content-be-protected-by-copyright); [Kluwer Copyright Blog roundup, 2026](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/copyright-blog/eu-copyright-law-roundup-first-trimester-of-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Human-set parameters.** A deterministic generator gives the user complete control over what appears: every visible element follows from a human choice of part and slider value. On the Copyright Office's own framing (degree of control, not predictability) this is the assistive side of the line, comparable to drawing or 3D software, and it meets the Japanese "tool" test and the EU "free and creative choices" test where the choices are genuinely creative. The claim is to the selection, coordination and arrangement, so protection is likely thin: trivial combinations (two or three choices, defaults) may fail the originality minimum, and others remain free to make similar characters from the same parts.
- **Layered ownership.** The part shapes are the part author's works. A composed character therefore contains the developer's (or contributors') artwork plus the user's selection. Under the "lion's share" cases the tool author does not own output merely by writing the program, but pre-drawn artwork embedded in output is a different matter from program logic. The repository licence should say explicitly that parts are CC0 or carry an output exception, as MakeHuman does; a share-alike or copyleft licence on part artwork could otherwise attach conditions to commercial characters.
- **Heavy AI-agent involvement.** If an agent picks parts and slider values from a brief instruction, the human has not determined the expressive elements: in the US that selection looks like prompting plus "authorship by adoption", which the Office says is insufficient; in Japan it resembles simple instructions. The claim strengthens with detailed human specification of expressive features, iterative human correction of specific parameters, human choice and arrangement across a cast, and human post-editing. Keep parameter files and edit history as evidence of human contribution.
- **What survives regardless.** Even a character whose parameter selection is not human-authored still embodies human-drawn parts (protected as the part author's work), and the commercial products built around it (card layouts, text, animation, story) are ordinary human works. Names and logos can be protected by trademark irrespective of authorship.
- [M] US registration practice (Copyright Office guidance of March 2023, 88 Fed. Reg. 16190) is recalled as requiring applicants to disclose and disclaim AI-generated content that is more than de minimis; the Federal Register page could not be fetched this session.

### Gaps
- No authority was found that addresses output of a parametric or procedural (non-AI) character creator, in any of the three jurisdictions. The Copyright Office's promised Compendium update and any further registration guidance were not located.
- Whether *Allen v. Perlmutter* has been decided after March 2026 is unknown.

## 8. Practical risk assessment and safe-practice rules

### Takeaway
The published artefacts (code, aggregate statistics, originally drawn parts) carry low copyright risk; the realistic exposures are, in descending order, parts or presets that recreate a specific character, breach of dataset or site terms, and the legality of the private corpus-building step, which is clearly lawful in Japan, conditionally lawful in the EU and a fair-use judgment call in the US. Nothing here has been tested in court for this exact activity, so the rules below aim to keep every step on the settled side of each doctrine.

### Cited Findings
- [P] Ideas, methods and facts are unprotected (§102(b), *Feist*); EU recital 9 says mining unprotected facts or data needs no authorisation. — [Cornell LII, §102](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/102); [Cornell LII, Feist](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340); [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [P] Japan's guidance flags three failure modes: targeting a small group of one creator's works to output their common creative expression; taking database works sold for analysis or circumventing robots.txt or login controls; knowingly collecting from sites distributing infringing content. — [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)
- [P] EU Art. 4 requires lawful access, no machine-readable reservation, and retention only "for as long as is necessary for the purposes of text and data mining". — [EUR-Lex, Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32019L0790)
- [S] The Third Circuit (29 Sept 2026) held that convenience does not justify intermediate copying and weighed an emerging data-licensing market against a commercial non-generative tool. — [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/what-thomson-reuters-v-ross-does-and-doesnt-say-now-about-fair-use-and-generative-ai/); [Ballard Spahr](https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/10/third-circuit-addresses-fair-use-in-ai-training-but-leaves-generative-ai-questions-unresolved)
- [S] *Bartz*: the source of copies matters; a library built from pirated copies was not fair use and led to a $1.5 billion settlement. — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/court-grants-final-approval-anthropic-copyright-settlement/)
- [P] A character is protected when "especially distinctive" with "unique elements of expression"; stock characters are not. — [Ninth Circuit, Towle (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2015/09/23/13-55484.pdf)
- [P] Manga109 is academic-only; Manga109-s permits commercial use of experiment results with acknowledgement and no redistribution. — [Manga109 project site](https://manga109.github.io/manga109-project-website/en/); [Hugging Face, Manga109-s](https://huggingface.co/datasets/hal-utokyo/Manga109-s)
- [P] pixiv prohibits crawler aggregation and prejudicial use of data-analysis results. — [pixiv terms](https://policies.pixiv.net/en.html)
- [P] Human control over expressive elements is what makes output protectable in the US; creative contribution in Japan. — [US Copyright Office Part 2 (PDF)](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf); [Agency for Cultural Affairs overview (PDF)](https://www.bunka.go.jp/english/policy/copyright/pdf/94055801_01.pdf)

### Inferences

**Risk ranking (researcher's assessment, not a legal opinion)**

| Element | Risk | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Publishing code and corpus-level statistics | Low | Facts and ideas; no work reproduced. Settled doctrine, though unlitigated for this use. |
| Originally drawn generic parts | Low | Author's own expression of stock features. |
| Parts or presets modelled on a specific character; character names | High | Reproduces protected expression; adds trademark and unfair-competition exposure. The most likely source of a real complaint. |
| Using academic-only or terms-restricted datasets | Medium to high | Contract breach independent of copyright; easy to prove from a public repository's own documentation. |
| Private downloading for measurement: Japan | Low | Within the text of Art. 30-4(ii), subject to the proviso. |
| Private downloading for measurement: EU | Low to medium | Art. 4 applies only absent opt-out and with lawful access; delete after use. |
| Private downloading for measurement: US | Medium, uncertain | Fair use is plausible but unlitigated; appellate reasoning from Sept 2026 is unfavourable to convenience copying by commercial tools. |
| Output resembling a famous character by chance | Low to medium | No derivation if the pipeline is aggregate-only, but access to the corpus undermines an independent-creation defence; commercial use raises trademark risk. |
| Enforceability of the user's copyright in output | Medium | Likely thin for human-set parameters; weak where an AI agent chose them. |

**Safe-practice rules**

1. **Aggregate widely.** Compute every published statistic over many works by many artists, studios and eras; set a minimum number of distinct artists per statistic and per principal-component analysis. Never fit to one artist, one series or one character. (Japan's "small group of works" warning; US market-dilution and trade-dress theories.)
2. **No per-character or per-work artefacts.** Do not store, publish or ship landmark sets, parameter vectors or presets tied to an identifiable existing character or drawing. Discard per-image measurements once aggregated, or keep them private and unlabelled.
3. **Prefer clean inputs.** In order: own and commissioned drawings (with a written licence covering analysis and commercial use of results); CC0 and compatible CC material; Manga109-s with an approved application and the required acknowledgement; proportion conventions from drawing manuals (take the convention, not the diagrams). Treat scraped boards and pixiv as last resorts.
4. **Do not use academic-only data** (full Manga109 and similar) for anything that feeds the published statistics. Record each dataset's licence text and date at the time of use.
5. **Respect access controls and reservations.** Honour robots.txt, rate limits, login walls and site terms; in the EU treat any machine-readable or terms-based reservation as an opt-out. Do not collect from sources known to host unauthorised copies where an authorised source exists.
6. **Minimise and do not redistribute copies.** Measure in memory where practical, retain images only as long as the analysis needs, keep the corpus private, and never publish images, thumbnails, crops or traced outlines.
7. **Author parts independently.** Draw parts from the aggregate statistics and the designer's own ideas, not from reference sheets of specific characters. Keep dated authorship records and source files for every part. If contributors are accepted, require a statement of originality.
8. **Name nothing after existing characters, series, studios or artists**: not parts, presets, palettes, file names, tags, test fixtures or gallery captions. Do not market the tool as producing a named artist's or franchise's look.
9. **Screen outputs before commercial use.** Check each commercial character against well-known characters (reverse image search and human review) for recognisable combinations of hair silhouette, colour scheme, costume and signature accessories; change it if a reasonable fan would name an existing character.
10. **License the part library for unencumbered output.** State in the repository that part artwork is CC0 or carries an explicit output exception and that composed characters belong to the user, following the MakeHuman model.
11. **Preserve evidence of human authorship.** Keep parameter files, edit history and notes showing human creative choices; where an AI agent proposes parameters, have a human make and record specific expressive decisions and post-edits. Expect to disclose AI involvement on US registration.
12. **Document provenance** in a short data statement: sources, licences, dates, counts of works and artists, what was retained and what was deleted. This is the main evidence for lawful analysis, independent creation and licence compliance.
13. **Protect the commercial characters with trademark as well as copyright** (names, logos), since copyright in parameter selections may be thin.
14. **Get jurisdiction-specific advice before commercial launch**, and re-check the pending matters listed at the top of these notes.

### Gaps
- The risk ranking is the researcher's synthesis; no source rates these risks for this activity, and no enforcement action against a comparable non-generative tool was found.
- Conflict-of-laws questions (which country's law governs downloading done in one country from servers in another, and distribution of a global open-source repository) were not researched.
- Moral rights, design rights, and personality or publicity rights in characters modelled on real people were outside the research and are not covered.
