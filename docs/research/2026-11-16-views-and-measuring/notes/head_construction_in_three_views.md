# Head construction in three views: side, three-quarter, model sheets, landmarks, animal ears

Research note, 2026-11-16. Evidence only, not a design (`EVID-1`, `EVID-2`, principles revision 2026-11-15.1, read first). Decision served: how the turned views get depth and outlines, which proportions and side-view guides a head needs, and what a reviewer is told to look for. Sufficient evidence: several independent professional sources agreeing. Appetite: about 60 fetches, spent.

## Summary

Found: side-view conventions that agree across independent Japanese lesson platforms (ear at or just behind the middle of the head's depth, between eye or brow level and nose level; lips on or inside the nose-to-chin line; neck leaning forward from under the ear), one official lesson with explicit three-quarter numbers (far eye 0.7 of the near eye, mouth 1:2), and Loomis's own numbers for a realistic head (side view in a square 3½ units each way, front view about 3 units wide). Not found: any anime-specific number for skull depth, for how far nose, lips and chin project or for neck angle, and any measured account of how drawn turned views depart from projection; every study of drawn faces located measured front views only. Not found either: Japanese studio documents, animator textbooks or interviews on keeping head views consistent, or any lesson fixing where round animal ears attach beyond "high on the head, a little behind a hairband line, tilted forward".

## 1. Side view (横顔)

Skull and depth
- [P] No lesson gives skull depth as a ratio. A: a circle is "頭蓋骨のイメージ". F: the skull in profile is a slightly wide ellipse (横長の楕円). C: the back of the head must at least contain a circle shown in its diagram ("最低限、青で示した円が入るくらいの大きさ"). H (あすかパン): add about half a radius to the head ("半径の1/2ほど盛ります"), called approximate by its author.

Level lines
- [P] A: "円の上から顎先までを...5等分", five lines: ① つむじ ② 髪の生え際 ③ 目 ④ 鼻の下 ⑤ 顎先, plus a cross through the circle's centre. The spacing is in the diagram. Inference: A also puts the eyes at "頭頂部から顎先までの半分あたり", so the five lines bound four equal intervals.
- [P] D: halving scheme, said to hold in every horizontal turn ("正面・横・斜めなど...比率は全て同じ"): brow at half crown-to-chin; hairline at half crown-to-brow; nose tip at half brow-to-chin; outer eye corner at half brow-to-nose-tip; mouth at half nose-tip-to-chin.
- [P] F: crown-to-eye equals eye-to-chin; hairline, brow, nose and chin spaced 1:1:1; mouth one third of the way from nose to chin. H (あすかパン) credits 1:1:1 to Loomis.

Ear
- [P] Front to back. C: "「おでこ～耳」の幅と「耳の付け根～後頭部」の幅がほぼ同じ". B: the circle's centre is "ほぼ耳の位置"; the ear sits in the back half, "十字線の中心から少しだけ外側". A: "耳は、円の十字線から生える". G: "right in the middle of the face (slightly outside)"; too far back makes the face look too big. E: on the vertical line at "おおよそ1/2の位置". H (MYAMA): where the face mask meets the sphere.
- [P] Levels. I: top rim level with the brow, bottom at the end of the nose; behind the jaw joint, on the extension of the jaw line, tilted; shōjo-style drawings often set it too low. A: between ③ and ④ (woman); brow to nose tip (man). B: eye and ear at the same height. E: between brow line and under-nose line.

Front profile line
- [P] A: forehead follows the circle between ② and ③; a vertical guide dropped from the forehead's most forward point; nose underside on ④; chin tip near ⑤ on a guide "中心線とおでこの中間あたり"; lips a little below ④. A's profile uses three vertical references (the circle's cross, a "centre line", the forehead) whose spacing is only in the diagram.
- [P] B: the eye sits on the horizontal cross line "円周から半径の3分の1ほど内側"; the nose starts at the deepest dent of the profile; the chin tip lies on a vertical dropped from the pupil ("円の左半分3分の1あたり") where it meets the circle; lips at "鼻の下と顎の中間地点".
- [P] C: the forehead should not jut; the line from the nose down is level with the forehead or ahead of it.
- [P] E-line. A: "「鼻先〜唇〜顎」を結ぶライン"; neither hollow nor chin thrust forward. C: "唇はこのラインに触れない程度"; lower lip a little behind the upper. F: lips inside the line. G: the line is diagonal; "chin sticking out in front" is a named mistake. H (JJWho): mouth behind the line. **Conflict:** B has the lips protrude slightly past it ("唇がわずかに飛び出す").
- [P] No source gives a projection distance for nose tip, lips or chin.

Jaw and neck
- [P] Jaw. A: from under the ear to the chin tip. E: an inverted triangle from the ear through the chin. F: a レ-shaped guide with the ear on its extension.
- [P] Neck. C: not vertical; "すこし前傾に斜めに". A: the front starts a little above the chin, "円の十字線と中心線の、中間あたり"; the nape starts midway between ear root and back of head, at lip height. H: directly under the ear root (MYAMA); about one third of head width, tilted forward (JJWho). E: pivot near the ear, a little behind the jaw. **Conflict:** Palmie lesson 13 (shori, male face, <https://www.palmie.jp/lessons/13>) draws it straight down. No angle in degrees anywhere.

Eye and brow
- [P] G: a ">" shape, half as visible as in front, pupil a thin oval. H (HanairoHinode): set back about half an eye from the edge. A: upper lashes on ③, lower lashes between ③ and ④; "目の横幅は、おでこから中心線までの中間あたりに収まる"; brow between ② and ③.

Women against men, girls and children
- [P] A: woman's nose flows from the forehead, not pointed; man's features sit slightly higher, with a flat forehead and straight bridge. G: lower eyes and a smaller chin read younger and more feminine. H: omitting the jaw corner reads younger (MYAMA); a mouth bulging past the E-line reads younger (あすかパン). C: for women and children the ear-to-jaw line is shaded, not drawn. No side-view numbers separate an adult woman from a girl.

Checks the lessons name
- [P] G's four mistakes: eye drawn as in front view, ear too far back, mouth drawn as in front view, chin jutting. C: back of head too small; forehead too far forward. E: one continuous centre line must pass through the finished face. C and いちあっぷ (<https://ichi-up.net/2015/037>): flip the drawing; draw glasses on it.

## 2. Three-quarter view (斜め顔)

- [P] Angle. No lesson defines 斜め in degrees. C lists practice views "正面向き、斜め30度、斜め60度、真横、斜め後ろ、後ろ向き" (a paid course, not explained). H (HanairoHinode) says "4分の3の角度". Nothing found on 30 against 45 degrees.
- [P] A, the only source with numbers:
  - the centre line is shifted sideways to set the turn; a second guide marks the side plane ("顔の側面を表す補助線");
  - near eye starts "中心線から1/4程度" of the centre-line-to-side-guide span (woman), "1/5程度" (man);
  - "目と目の間の距離は、中心線から手前側の目頭までの距離を「1」として、その半分程度" (ambiguous without the diagram);
  - far eye: "手前を「1」とすると、奥側は「0.7」くらい";
  - mouth at the top quarter between ④ and the chin; width "「奥側：1」「手前側：2」" (woman), "奥側1：手前側3" (man), presumably either side of the centre line;
  - ear where the side guide meets the neck line, between ③ and ④;
  - far cheek: place the cheekbone, then a rounded line from the chin up to the side guide.
- [P] D: seen from above the face is a gentle curve, so the far eye narrows, more at its outer corner ("目尻側を強く圧縮して、目頭側は弱めに"); nose and mouth follow the centre line. No ratio.
- [P] B: on a turned head the cross lines bend with the sphere; eye and ear keep their places; the chin tip stays on the brow-to-nose line.
- [P] E: the centre line sets the gaze; the chin triangle sits where a vertical from the ear meets the sphere; level lines run parallel to the sphere's equator and arc with tilt; thickness is added behind the sphere for the back of the head.
- [P] H: far eye "わずかに小さく、より細く", eyes one eye apart (HanairoHinode); centre line nearer the side, nose protruding a little past it (JJWho).
- [P] Departure from geometry, stated but not quantified. H (ユキノコ): cheeks and forehead "may be reduced to make the eyes larger"; it is "better to prioritize rich emotional expression over strict three-dimensional correctness".
- [P, a hobbyist's public note, not a professional source] Faces drawn at 30 to 45 degrees came out too long; reasoning from a 3:2 ellipse seen from above, the face's visible width stays near constant up to 45 degrees, then falls about linearly — <https://scrapbox.io/appbirdNotebook-public/顔の角度と見える横幅の関係>
- Inference: 0.7 is close to cos 45° (0.71), not cos 30° (0.87), so A's far-eye number fits a flat projection at about 45 degrees. A 1:2 mouth cannot come from turning a flat face under orthographic projection (that gives 1:1); it needs a curved face, as D says, or a drawing habit.

## 3. Production model sheets

- [P] Clip Studio's Art Rocket, Julio Robledo (freelance character designer, not an anime studio), <https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/164740>. Head turnaround: front, profile, three-quarter, rear. Profile and front come first; guide lines for eye centres, chin, eyebrows and shoulders are carried across; the three-quarter is sketched between them; the rear is derived from the front. Construction sheet: profile inside a square divided in four, eye and pupil on its horizontal half, nose outside the square, ear level with the nose and the top of the pupil.
- [P, generic retellings citing no studio] A vendor blog (<https://blog.cg-wire.com/character-sheet-animation>): front, profile, back, three-quarters "midway between the front and side views", horizontal guidelines. An encyclopedia (<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_sheet>): head and body at various angles; how tightly a production holds to the model varies by studio.
- [P, OCR text] Preston Blair, *Advanced Animation* (American cartoon animation), <https://archive.org/details/advancedanimation>: "Think of the head as a rounded mass, either ball shaped, pear shaped or egg-shaped"; "Perspective lines around these masses are put in to establish front, side, tilt". No proportions.
- [P] Japanese lessons call the head a solid without giving proportions: "球体状の頭部に、平面のお面が張り付くイメージ" (いちあっぷ 2015); a box with front, side and top faces (A, D).
- [P, abstracts only] Two graphics papers treat per-view correction as normal practice and give no figures: 2D deformations that "do not need to be tied to an underlying object" (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05544>); contour-driven rig fitting to end a "laborious trial-and-error workflow" (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00732>).

## 4. Loomis's ball and plane (realistic heads)

Andrew Loomis, *Drawing the Head and Hands* (1956), OCR text of <https://archive.org/details/andrew-loomis-drawing-the-head-hands> [P]. These are averages for a realistic adult head: "The standard proportions for a man's head".

- Ball "flattened somewhat at the sides". Plate 1: "divide the ball into quarters and again at the equator", then "slice off a fairly thin slice on each side". No fraction is given for the slice.
- "The 'equator' becomes the brow line." "About halfway up from the brow line to the axis, we establish the hairline." Down the middle line, "two points about equal to the space of the forehead" give the nose length and the bottom of the chin.
- Jaw line "connects about halfway around the ball on each side". "The ears attach along the halfway line (up and down) at a distance about equal to the space of from the brows to the bottom of the nose."
- Units (Plates 18, 19). Front view: a rectangle "three units of measurement wide, and three and a half deep". "The side view of the head fits exactly into a square three and one-half units in each direction." The head is "nearly three units wide (to include the ears), and three and one-half units from tip of nose to the back of the head". "Ears, nose to brow, lips and chin are each one unit." The eyes are "at the halfway division of the whole head".
- Inference: depth (nose tip to back of head) equals height; width with ears is about 0.86 of height.
- Women (Plate 40): proportions "vary only slightly from those of the male head"; brows "a little higher above the eyes"; mouth smaller, eyes "slightly larger".
- "In profile the head is not like an egg."
- [P, third-party retelling] Proko adds what Loomis's text does not state: the side-plane oval is "⅔ of the height of the circle"; the jaw corner falls about halfway into the oval — <https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-head-from-any-angle>
- Inference: E follows this construction step for step (polar axis, shaved sides, equator as brow line, equal units) without naming it.

## 5. Landmark vocabulary

Anthropometric definitions
- [P] Traditional definitions "following Farkas (1994)", from Katina et al., *Journal of Anatomy* (2015), <https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91512/14/Katina_et_al-Journal_of_Anatomy.pdf>:
  - nasion: "The mid-point on the soft tissue contour of the base of the nasal root at the level of the frontonasal suture";
  - pronasale: "The most anterior mid-point of the nasal tip"; subnasale: "The point where the nasal septum merges with the upper cutaneous lip in the mid-sagittal plane";
  - stomion: "The mid-point of the horizontal labial fissure"; cheilion: "The point located at each labial commissure";
  - gnathion: "The most anterior-inferior mid-point of the chin";
  - exocanthion / endocanthion: "The soft tissue point located at the outer commissure" / "inner commissure of each eye fissure";
  - tragion: "The point located at the upper margin of each tragus".
  The paper calls such definitions two-dimensional in origin and gnathion the hardest to place.
- [P, table in a 2025 F1000Research paper retelling standard definitions; read through Europe PMC] <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12334920/>: vertex "The highest point on the head"; trichion "Anterior hairline at the mid-line"; glabella "The most prominent point between the eyebrows, above the nasal root"; gonion "The most lateral and posterior point of the mandibular angle"; gnathion "The lowest point on the mandibular symphysis" (differs from Katina).

Studies of drawn faces (all front view)
- [P] Liu, Chen and Chang 2019, *Symmetry* 11(5):664, <https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/symmetry/symmetry-11-00664/article_deploy/symmetry-11-00664.pdf>: 26 lettered "location points" on a front face; the vertical chain is p (hairline), a (between the brows), c (nasal floor), e (lip line), g (chin); widths of skull, face, nose, mandible, jaw and ear. Ear length was often "covered by hair", so only ear width was taken. Coordinates from the Photoshop CS6 measuring tool. No depth or profile measure.
- [P] Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022, *Anthropological Review* 85(2), <https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/download/13199/15130?inline=1>: "en face" images in ImageJ; landmarks after Martin and Saller (1957): pu (pupil centre), en, ex, prn, sto, gn ("lower edge of the mandible (in the midline)"). Where eye corners were not closed, en and ex went to "the points that were most inward (en) or lateral (ex)". Bone-related points "(such as glabella, zygion, frontotemporale, gonion) were excluded because there was a higher risk of their incorrect placement"; lip corners and eyebrow points were excluded as expression-dependent. Lengths divided by pu–pu.
- [P] Stricker, Augereau, Kise and Iwata 2018, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03214>: 60 points adapted from the 68-point iBUG scheme: 5 per eyebrow, 10 per eye, 1 per pupil, 1 for the nose tip, 10 along the mouth line, 17 on the chin contour. "The corners of the eyes and mouth are not drawn. The bottom part of the nose is also missing"; the nose is "usually simplified in a line"; eye points are spread evenly because the lid intersection "is rarely drawn". Profile faces were removed: "We decided to focus on frontal faces".

Which points survive on a stylised drawing (Inference from these three papers)
- Used without trouble: pupil centre, pronasale as "nose tip", stomion as the midpoint of the mouth line, gnathion as the lowest midline chin point, the chin and jaw contour, the hairline midpoint, the point between the brows.
- Redefined: eye corners become the most medial and most lateral points of the drawn eye.
- Dropped or unreliable: nasion (no bridge), subnasale (Stricker: the bottom of the nose is missing; Liu nevertheless marks a "nasal floor"), cheilion, the lip points, bone-based points such as glabella, zygion and gonion.
- Not used on drawings in these studies: vertex, tragion and any profile-only point.

## 6. Animal ears on a human-shaped head

- [P] Palmie lesson 43, 冬空実 (social-game and light-novel illustrator), <https://www.palmie.jp/lessons/43>: ears standing perpendicular on the head are the bad example; "通常、動物の耳はこのように前に傾いています". A cat ear is two triangles with a step. "耳と頭は完全に独立しておらず、後頭部から耳まで、なだらかな流れで繋がっています." In front view the ears turn slightly outward. Set the ear "前髪と後ろ髪の間に入れるような気持ち". A strongly deformed style may skip this.
- [P] いちあっぷ, あおいサクラ子 (freelance illustrator), 2017, <https://ichi-up.net/2017/103>: "動物の耳は横から見ると後頭部からなだらかについており、前方に倒れたシルエット"; a tube with thickness, not a flat triangle.
- [P] Palmie lesson 329 (2024, no named instructor), <https://www.palmie.jp/lessons/329>: ears high on the head; picture a hairband and grow the ears "カチューシャの少し後ろの位置から"; this is "あくまで目安", and "獣人は架空の生物なので、お好みの骨格でデザインしてもOK".
- [P] MediBang Paint, アニー, 2023, <https://medibangpaint.com/en/use/2023/05/cute-animal-ears/>: rabbit ears near the curve of the top of the head, on the line that "divides the front and back of the head" in side view; cat ears "closer to the sides of the head". "If you draw them vertically, the ears will look like headbands". Length against face height: rabbit two thirds, cat one third, fox one half. The forward tilt is easy to show from the side, harder from the front.
- [P] Human ears. MediBang Paint, つかさん, 2022, <https://medibangpaint.com/en/use/2022/07/how-to-characters-having-animal-ears>: "sometimes I avoid drawing human ears so we don't have to draw four ears"; bear: "The round ears are characteristic". CLIP STUDIO TIPS, 黑色林檎, <https://tips.clip-studio.com/ja-jp/articles/9018>: "元々の人間の耳の部分は毛で覆う必要があります"; "人間の耳の位置に獣耳を描くデザインもあります".
- Inference: the agreed points are high placement, forward tilt, a root flowing from the back of the head, and human ears hidden or left out. The habit named as wrong is the upright, headband-like ear.
