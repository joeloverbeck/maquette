# Measuring drawings without detectors

Research note, 2026-11-16. Decision served: whether landmark positions "measured by an agent, checked by the owner's eyes" can be trusted to a few percent of head height, what validation that needs first, and how the checking can be made cheap. Evidence under its own conditions (rules read: `EVID-1`, `EVID-2`, principles revision 2026-11-15.1). 46 page fetches and 15 searches, plus registry and metadata lookups.

Marking: **[P]** the source that owns the finding was opened and read; **[S]** search summary or retelling; **Inference** is my reasoning.

## Summary

No published source measures how accurately a general multimodal model places landmarks on anime or line-art faces, and the earlier claim of "about 2 source pixels" has no published support. The nearest evidence (plots, screenshots, pointing benchmarks) supports typical errors of a few percent of the plotted range or image extent on unambiguous targets, with a substantial share of gross misses and systematic pulls toward ticks and expected patterns that an average hides. Every source that could state an accuracy had ground truth independent of the reader, and the hand-measurement studies of drawn faces mostly report no repeatability, so the method needs a named validation step (known-truth images, repeat readings, a second rater on a subset) before its numbers are used.

## 1. How drawn faces were measured by hand

- **Stricker et al. 2018** [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.03214): 60 landmarks per frontal manga face (5 per eyebrow, 10 per eye, 1 per pupil, 1 nose, 10 mouth, 17 chin contour), adapted from the iBUG photo-face scheme because eye corners, nostrils and lips are often not drawn. 1,446 faces kept from 5,505 after removing profile views and faces under 80 px wide or high. "656 images were labeled twice. If the distance between two landmarks was greater than 2 pixels, then these landmarks were manually corrected and compared again"; the two labelings were then averaged. Error is divided by the distance between the first and last chin-contour landmark, not the inter-eye distance, because "Manga eyes proportions and sizes vary a lot". The largest remaining mean disagreement between two labelings of one image was 0.0333 of that distance. Their trained detector's best mean error was 0.02935, with 19.31% of test images above 0.0333. Tool and number of annotators: not stated.
- **Bisiecka and Borysławski 2022** [P] (https://czasopisma.uni.lodz.pl/ar/article/view/13199): 173 "en face" images (86 male, 87 female characters) from 90 series; landmarks placed in ImageJ: pupil centre, inner and outer eye corner, nose tip, stomion, gnathion, chosen as "easiest to identify on the simplified facial model"; where eye corners are not drawn closed, the most inward and lateral points were used. "The measurements were divided by reference measurement pu-pu" (pupil-centre distance). Number of people placing landmarks: not stated. Repeatability: none reported. Faces had to be "as motionless as possible"; no tilt check is described.
- **Ota 2024** [P] (https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kaogaku/24/1/24_3/_article/-char/ja): 64 characters (32 male, 32 female), front-facing neutral design images; average faces made in WebMorph with 138 points marked per face; eye size measured as the eye's aspect ratio relative to the face's. The study rates attractiveness; it gives no proportion table, rater count or repeatability.
- **Liu, Chen and Chang 2019** [P, abstract only, through Crossref] (https://doi.org/10.3390/sym11050664): 100 characters from American and Japanese animation described "using angles, lengths, and areas" against real faces; eye area twice (American) and 3.4 times (Japanese) the human value. 26 locating points [S]. The publisher and DOAJ refused automated fetches (HTTP 403); tool, divisors, raters and repeatability were not read. The 2020 follow-up [P, abstract] (https://doi.org/10.3390/sym12081261) took 20 body-part lengths from 90 + 90 characters.
- **Others**: Ashari et al. 2025 [P, abstract] (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-82677-0) sorted 88 film characters into jaw-profile classes by judgement, without landmarks. Jha et al. 2018: 750 caricature faces, 15 landmarks [S, retold by Stricker].
- **Photo-anthropometry practice**
  - Campomanes-Álvarez et al. [P] (https://doi.org/10.1007/s00414-014-1002-4): 39 operators, 18 landmarks, 10 photographs (frontal, oblique, lateral), marking only points they could place precisely; 3 operators repeated 13 points on 5 photographs 5 times at least 24 h apart. Dispersion is each mark's deviation from the across-observer mean, scaled 0 to 1 and tested by ANOVA; zygion, gonion and gnathion were significantly harder, and their dispersion depended on pose. No error in millimetres or percent. It retells Cummaudo et al. 2013 (24 operators; 3 repeating 20 times) as showing "only very few landmarks seem to be reliable" [S for Cummaudo], and names "differences in head orientation" as an error source.
  - Ulijaszek and Kerr 1999 [P, abstract page] (https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/402C85D95FBDB910A21ED28874775BC2): imprecision is reported as technical error of measurement (TEM), relative TEM and reliability coefficient R, which are "mathematically interrelated"; "R > 0·95 should be sought where possible". Perini et al. 2005 [P, text] (https://doi.org/10.1590/S1517-86922005000100009): 20 subjects measured twice by one person (intra) and by pairs of people (inter). Its formulas and threshold table are images I could not read; the usual form TEM = √(Σd²/2n) is [S].
  - Koo and Li 2016 [P] (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4913118/): report ICC model, type and definition with the 95% interval; above 0.90 is "excellent", judged on the interval; "at least 30 heterogeneous samples and ... at least 3 raters whenever possible".
  - A 2020 comparison of 2D photographs with 3D imaging [P] (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7078411): 50 subjects, 2 observers, each image landmarked twice 4 weeks apart; ICC 0.96–0.99 between and ≥0.99 within observers across modalities, yet "2D photographs displayed the highest combined total error for linear measurements".
- Inference: none of the drawn-face proportion studies reports repeatability; only the manga dataset double-annotated. ICCs of 0.96 and above were reported alongside the largest total error, so a per-landmark distance error bears on "a few percent", not ICC alone. All studies avoided tilt by selecting frontal images; none measured it. A rigid pitch of θ moves a point lying z in front of the rotation axis by z·sin θ in the image (17% of z at 10°), so face-plane features shift against crown and chin.

## 2. How accurately multimodal models place a point

- **Anthropic documentation** [P] (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision-coordinates, and `/vision`): "Claude's coordinate and localization outputs are approximate ... verify outputs before relying on them." Coordinates are pixels in the image *after* resizing; one visual token per 28×28 px patch; limits 1,568 px edge and 1,568 tokens (standard tier), 2,576 px and 4,784 tokens (high-resolution tier); a 1920×1080 image becomes 1456×819 on the standard tier, and overlooking this "is the most common cause of misaligned coordinates". "Small elements lose precision when an image is downscaled: for fine targets, crop the region of interest and send the crop". No accuracy figure.
- **Google documentation** [P] (https://aistudio.google.com/docs/image-understanding, `/docs/robotics-overview`): boxes and points are normalised to 0–1000, y before x; advice to crop or zoom small objects and, "for high-precision tasks, query multiple times and average the results". No accuracy figure.
- **PointArena** [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09990): 982 image-query pairs on photographs; a point is correct if it lies "within the target mask". GPT-4o 29.5%, Gemini-2.5-Flash 46.8%; step-by-step reasoning lowered both (to 26.6% and 30.9%). No distance error.
- **MolmoPoint** [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.28069): on the same benchmark, human 89.1%, best model 70.7%, Gemini-2.5-Pro 62.8%. Its point output has "a precision of about 4.7 pixels" by construction.
- **ScreenSpot-Pro** [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07981): 1,581 high-resolution screenshots; targets average 0.07% of screen area; a hit is a point inside the target box. GPT-4o 0.8%, best specialist 18.9% in 2025. Later figures on it [P, MolmoPoint]: Claude 3.7 27.7%, Gemini-3-Pro 72.7%, best open model 61.1%.
- **Error as a distance (plots only)**
  - Polak and Morgan 2025 [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12326): Claude 3.5 Sonnet reading data points from plots; precision and recall about 89–92%; mean absolute error 2.7–2.9% of data range in x and 1.0–2.6% in y. Main error: continuing an expected pattern ("4.1" reported as "4").
  - Scatterplot benchmark (Palmeiro et al. 2025) [P] (https://research.feedzai.com/oshuzuxa/2025/10/VIS_x_GenAI__Benchmark_Paper__camera_ready_version_accessible.pdf): OpenAI and Google models asked for pixel coordinates on roughly 600 px images; a match is within 10 px; "Precision and Recall are near or below 50%", best 65.01%.
  - PlotGround 2026 [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06825): 1,119 values read from real figures, truth from the authors' source tables. Within ±5% of the value: best model 87.5%, Claude Opus 5 77.1%, Claude Sonnet 5 74.4%; within ±2%: 73.8%, 60.2%, 55.6%. A coding agent that repeatedly cropped and enlarged and measured pixel positions reached 90.0% within ±5%, median relative error 0.39% among those. "Predictions often concentrate on labeled tick values."
- **Line drawings** [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06581): on seven tasks over drawn lines, circles and grids, four 2024 models averaged 58.07% (best 77.84%); errors rose as shapes came close together. The authors describe models as "unable to 'zoom in' to extract details".
- **Anime, illustration or sketch landmarks:** two searches found no evaluation of a general multimodal model.
- Inference: a hit-inside-region score cannot be converted to a distance. The distance figures (1–3% of range on plots; 56–88% of readings within 2–5%) are for marks with printed axes as built-in rulers, and the share of misses runs from about one in ten to more than half. A drawn face has softer landmark definitions and no axes, so transfer is unproven in either direction.

## 3. Which aids improve it

- **Zoom and crop** [P]: ScreenSpot-Pro, one specialist model: 18.9% direct, 31.0% iterative zooming, 31.9% iterative narrowing, 40.2% crop-and-reground, 48.1% full search. Iterative narrowing on ScreenSpot (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13591): a generalist rose from 42.89% to 69.1%, a specialist from 82.47% to 83.33%; some splits fell (90.87% to 86.96%) through "loss of contextual information". Both vendors advise cropping.
- **Numbered marks (Set-of-Mark)** [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11441): GPT-4V asked for coordinates directly scored 25.7 on a referring task; choosing among numbered marks on pre-segmented regions scored 86.4. It needs candidate regions made by another tool, and open models "can hardly interpret the marks".
- **Coordinate grids** [P]: a labelled 6×6 dot matrix (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12058) raised GPT-4V from 45.7 to 55.3 on average over 11 spatial-reasoning benchmarks; no coordinate accuracy was measured, and the model "occasionally struggles to accurately associate textual reasoning with the nearest coordinates". A 9×9 line grid (https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18270) raised box overlap from 0.27 to 0.56 on 500 photographs; the model is unnamed, and the authors warn of interference on images "containing similar geometric patterns".
- **Normalised coordinates** [P]: model-specific. Anthropic: "Claude does not work well when you ask for normalized coordinates"; Google's models are documented in 0–1000.
- **Drawing the model's points back for correction**
  - [P] https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13156: an untrained model shown its own rendered box fell from 79.6% to 17.0% at the last step; after training, iterating still did not beat a single answer without an oracle.
  - [P] https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23746: a model trained to refine its own drawn point gained 67.108 to 67.617 over three steps.
  - [P] https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06510: unaided self-review changed accuracy by −17.41 to +3.68 points; a model verifier +0.39 to +7.36; truthful yes/no feedback +6.14 to +23.91.
  - Candidate-selection loops [P] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07872): "strong performance even in the first iteration with modest improvement over further iterations".
- Inference: zooming has the best-measured gains. Grids are measured only for coarse tasks, and the tick-snapping result suggests a grid could pull readings toward its lines. A redraw loop is supported when the feedback comes from a reliable outside judge, not from the model re-reading its own marks. None of this was tested on late-2026 frontier models or on drawings.

## 4. Human verification designs

- **Double annotation with a disagreement threshold** [P, Stricker]: 45% of images labelled twice; pairs more than 2 px apart re-examined; remainder averaged.
- **Accept or nudge** [P] (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6899221/): markers start at the model's estimate and the person drags them; labelling time fell "from 2 min per frame for the first 10 frames to 6 s per frame for the last 500 frames" with 32 points per frame.
- **Verification only** [P, abstracts] (https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08405, https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02750): yes/no verification of proposed boxes cut annotation time "by a factor 6x-9x"; four clicks took 7 s against 35 s for a drawn box.
- **Human repeatability on images** [P]: one annotator relabelling the same video frames a month later differed by 2.69 ± 0.1 px RMSE, on frames of 640×480 to about 800×800 px (https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03142). Human-pose scoring constants were "computed from a set of 5000 redundantly annotated images", and at the strict threshold annotators themselves disagree about 30% of the time (https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05388).
- Inference: proposing points and letting a person drag them is the cheapest measured design, and its output is itself a second rating. No source measures how well a person who only looks at overlaid points, without clicking, notices errors of a few percent.

## 5. Small samples

- **Tolerance intervals** [P, formula] (https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc263.htm): "limits within which we expect a stated proportion of the population to lie", valid "if the data are from a normally distributed population". Inference (computed from that formula): to cover 95% of the population with 95% confidence the half-width is 5.09 SD at n = 5, 3.38 at n = 10, 2.95 at n = 15; for 90% coverage 4.27, 2.84, 2.48.
- **Mean with range**, Inference from order statistics (not opened): the sample range covers on average (n−1)/(n+1) of the population: 67% at n = 5, 82% at 10, 87.5% at 15. The chance that 15 samples span at least 90% of the population is 45%.
- **Principal components** [P, abstract] (https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.3827): with more dimensions than samples, leading directions are consistent only when their eigenvalues are large enough; "most other PC directions are strongly inconsistent". Inference: n samples give at most n−1 modes.
- **Shape models** [P] (Mei et al. 2008, https://iacl.ece.jhu.edu/proceedings/miccai2008/52410425.pdf): keeping modes by percentage of variance is "unreliable"; 2D faces with 22 points needed about 50 samples for 24 stable modes; 3D faces about 150; 319 samples were not enough for a 527-point heart model.
- **Convex hull** [P, abstract] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.09485): above 100 dimensions a new sample "almost surely never" falls inside the hull of the data. Inference: with 5–15 examples and many ratios, hull membership rejects nearly everything and independent per-ratio ranges accept unmeasured combinations. No source found says which bound is honest at this size.
