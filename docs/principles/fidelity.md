# Fidelity to externally governed sources

The core constitution's requirements language, definitions and applicable rules govern this module. Its Modules table determines any additional applicable modules. One adopted revision binds the core and its modules.

## Definitions

**Externally governed fidelity promise** is a claim that a repository will reproduce, conform to, or remain compatible with behavior controlled by a source set outside the repository's own design authority, such as a rulebook, protocol, formal standard, law or regulation, published interface or file format, or legacy system behavior.

**Fidelity obligation topology** is a coarse, source-derived map of the behavioral and adjudication families that determine an externally governed fidelity promise: controlling sources and precedence, base behaviors, exception classes, timing and lifecycle boundaries, persistent modifiers, optional or compound continuations, cross-rule interactions, unresolved ambiguity classes, and the oracles needed to decide them. It estimates obligation shape and burden; it is not an exhaustive state-space or pairwise-interaction matrix unless the accepted claim independently requires one.

### FID-1 — Source authority and feasibility precede fidelity implementation

**Trigger:** Risk-bearing work promises reproduction, conformance, or compatibility with externally governed behavior and is about to enter TDD, implementation, exact-candidate review, `ready-for-agent`, AFK or unattended execution.

**Rule:** When the promised output includes an externally governed fidelity promise, execution readiness additionally requires the exact controlling source set and revisions or equivalent immutable identities; precedence among those sources; the named authority or decision rule for ambiguity; failure behavior when no supported interpretation can be established; and an independent fidelity oracle. Derive and accept a fidelity obligation topology independently of the candidate architecture, then estimate implementation, authoring, adjudication, testing, review, and maintenance burden against appetite. Independently select the first hostile slice and its acceptance criteria under `FID-2`. Counts of cards, rules, sections, endpoints, commands, files, or other named items are not scope estimates when exceptions or interactions multiply obligations.

Unresolved source ambiguity blocks an unqualified fidelity claim. Before escalation, the agent MUST exhaust the controlling source set, precedence, reconciliation rules, and proportionate independent evidence, then recommend the best-supported local interpretation, variant, or reduced claim under `AGENT-5` even when no option can be presented as source fact. The acceptance authority MAY ratify that clearly labeled choice and continue within the narrower claim; it MUST NOT present the choice as source-verified fidelity. A bounded provisional implementation MAY proceed before ratification as decision-only work only when the interpretation remains explicit, local, reversible, quarantined from authoritative source fact and dependent production, and is surfaced in the next coherent acceptance surface. If those conditions do not hold, the blocker MUST name the human-governed consequence, recommendation, evidence limit, and safe return point under `AUTH-3` and `AGENT-5`.

Before the source set and fidelity obligation topology are accepted and the hostile slice is selected, only bounded source reconciliation, research, disposable local experiments, and decision-only probes MAY proceed. Their implementation MUST NOT gain dependent infrastructure, become canonical product machinery, authorize a generalized engine, DSL, framework, or inventory-scale product transcription or implementation, or count as product progress. Decision artifacts such as accepted source identities, ambiguity rulings, and the topology MAY become authoritative for the feasibility decision. This reconnaissance MAY be as deep as necessary within its appetite to discover the obligation surface; its implementation remains replaceable and subordinate to the decision.

### FID-2 — The first fidelity slice challenges the representation

**Trigger:** An externally governed fidelity promise is proposed.

**Rule:** For an externally governed fidelity promise, the first acceptance-bearing slice MUST be selected from independently derived hard cases and be hostile to the proposed representation or execution model. Where the fidelity obligation topology contains material cross-cutting interactions, the slice MUST exercise at least one such interaction through the real authoring, authority, execution, and oracle path. A tutorial, known script, simple scalar adjustment, isolated rule, per-item transcription milestone, or happy-path example is a seam probe unless it is itself the selected primary outcome. This requirement MUST NOT manufacture interactions absent from the governing source; the hostile slice is proportionate to the actual topology.

### FID-3 — Fidelity inventories describe behavioral obligations

**Trigger:** A representation may constrain an externally governed fidelity promise.

**Rule:** For an externally governed fidelity promise, the inventory required by `REP-2` MUST describe behavioral obligations, not only named source items. It MUST include the controlling sources, base behavior families, exception classes, precedence, timing and lifecycle boundaries, persistent modifiers, optional or compound continuations, and material interaction classes identified by the fidelity obligation topology; unresolved interpretations and untested interaction classes remain explicit deficits. A complete transcription or one test or receipt per card, endpoint, command, section, file, or other named item does not establish fidelity while cross-cutting obligations remain unrepresented or lack an independent oracle. The fidelity obligation topology MUST remain coarse but sufficient to expose material semantic, adjudication, authoring, and verification burden; it MUST NOT become an exhaustive pairwise matrix or state-space enumeration unless the accepted claim, safety case, or formal standard independently requires that stronger proof.

### FID-4 — Fidelity and utility need separate evidence

**Trigger:** Source fidelity or consumer utility is offered as evidence or grounds for adoption.

**Rule:** Evidence of source fidelity and evidence of consumer utility are separate claims. Reproducing selected source behavior does not establish that the implementation is useful, usable, maintainable, or worth its burden; helping a consumer does not establish fidelity to the governing source. When both claims justify the repository, each MUST have its own acceptance evidence and failure disposition. The acceptance authority MAY accept one and reject, narrow, park, or abandon the other without creating a duty to continue.

Cross-repository handoffs also trigger `INT-1` through `INT-4` as applicable.
